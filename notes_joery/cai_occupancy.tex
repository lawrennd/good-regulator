\section{The optimality event, derived}

The aim is an optimality variable built the way control as inference builds one: a per-step event with a likelihood given by the model, not a functional inserted by hand. The event we use is \emph{the regulator predicts its outcome}.

\paragraph{The event.} The regulator carries a predictive model $q$ of its outcome. At each step $t$ the channel emits $z_t \sim K(\cdot \mid s_t,a_t)$ and the model issues one guess $\hat z_t \sim q$. Set $O_t = 1$ when the guess is right. For discrete $Z$,
\begin{equation}
  p(O_t = 1 \mid z_t;\, q) = q(z_t), \qquad
  p(O_t = 1 \mid s_t,a_t;\, q) = \sum_z K(z \mid s_t,a_t)\, q(z).
  \label{eq:event}
\end{equation}
This is a probability for any $q$, so nothing needs normalising.

\paragraph{Reference measure as tolerance.} For continuous $Z$, call the guess right when it lands in a cell $C_\varepsilon(z_t)$ of $\mu$-mass $\varepsilon$ around $z_t$. Then $p(O_t=1\mid z_t) = Q(C_\varepsilon(z_t)) = \varepsilon\,\frac{dq}{d\mu}(z_t)\,(1+o(1))$. So $\mu$ is the resolution at which a prediction counts as correct, and $\ln \frac{dq}{d\mu}$ appears without being put in.

\paragraph{The trajectory model.} Discounting enters as in \citet{Levine-reinforcement18}, through termination with probability $1-\gamma$ per step. Under the prior policy $\pi_0$, the dynamics $P$, the channel $K$ and the events $O_{0:T}$, the joint over a trajectory $\tau = (s_t,a_t,z_t)_t$ is
\begin{equation}
  p(\tau, O_{0:T}=1;\, q) = \nu(s_0) \prod_t \pi_0(a_t\mid s_t)\, K(z_t\mid s_t,a_t)\, P(s_{t+1}\mid s_t,a_t)\; \prod_t q(z_t).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Variational posterior with nature fixed.} Take the variational family that keeps $\nu$, $P$ and $K$ and varies only the policy, $p_\pi(\tau)$. This is the variational fix that removes the optimism of exact inference: the posterior may not bend the dynamics or the channel towards the event. The evidence lower bound is
\begin{equation}
  \ln p(O=1;\, q) \;\ge\; \E_{p_\pi}\Big[\sum_t \ln q(z_t)\Big] - \mathrm{KL}\big(p_\pi \,\|\, p_{\pi_0}\big)
  \;=\; \tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\Big( \E_{z\sim \dpi K} \ln q(z) \;-\; \E_{s \sim \dpi} \mathrm{KL}\big(\pi(\cdot\mid s)\,\|\,\pi_0(\cdot\mid s)\big) \Big).
  \label{eq:elbo}
\end{equation}
The outcome enters only through the occupancy, because $\sum_{s,a} \dpi(s,a) K(z\mid s,a) = p_\pi(z)$.

\paragraph{Variational EM.} The E-step maximises \eqref{eq:elbo} over $\pi$ for fixed $q$: soft RL with reward $r_q(s,a) = \sum_z K(z\mid s,a) \ln q(z)$. The M-step maximises over $q$ for fixed $\pi$, and $\max_q \E_{p_\pi} \ln q$ is attained at $q = p_\pi$ with value $-H[p_\pi]$ by Gibbs' inequality. At the joint optimum,
\begin{equation}
  \max_{\pi, q} \text{\eqref{eq:elbo}} \;=\; \tfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\max_\pi \Big( -\Hmu[\dpi K] - \E_{s\sim\dpi} \mathrm{KL}(\pi\,\|\,\pi_0) \Big) + \text{const}.
\end{equation}
The entropy is not inserted. It is the optimal log-likelihood of the prediction event, once the regulator's model is learned by maximum likelihood. The M-step is empirical Bayes on the optimality likelihood, and the loop is the MM loop we already have. Tempering the event, $q(z_t)^{\beta}$, weights the entropy against the policy KL, and $\beta\to\infty$ recovers the unregularised objective and its deterministic vertex.

\paragraph{Tabular check.} On 30 random MDPs, with 5 states, 2 actions, 4 outcomes, a stochastic channel and $\gamma = 0.9$, the loop was run with soft value iteration as the E-step, a uniform $\pi_0$ and $\beta = 200$. The best of 5 random initial models reached the brute-force minimum of $H[\dpi K]$ over deterministic policies in 27 of 30 MDPs. Single runs stop at local optima, as expected for a concave minimisation. Script: \texttt{notes\_joery/scripts/cai\_check.py}.

\section{The order of the entropy is set by the event}

The same event, scored differently, gives different Rényi entropies $H_\alpha$.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{One guess, model chosen to win.} $P(O=1) = \langle p_\pi, q\rangle$, maximised over $q$ at the mode: $\max_z p_\pi(z) = e^{-H_\infty[p_\pi]}$.
\item \emph{One guess, drawn from the true outcome law.} $q = p_\pi$ gives $\sum_z p_\pi(z)^2 = e^{-H_2[p_\pi]}$, the probability that two independent outcomes coincide. This is the exact-likelihood version, and Toussaint's EM applies to it as written \citep{Toussaint-probabilistic06,Toussaint-expectation11}. $\sum p^2$ is convex in $d$, so the vertex survives, and its MM step is RL with reward $p_k(z)$.
\item \emph{Many guesses, log-likelihood, model learned.} The derivation above: $\max_q \E_{p_\pi} \ln q = -H_1[p_\pi]$, Shannon.
\end{itemize}
Every order is minimised by a concentrated outcome law, and they weight the tail differently, so they can pick different optima where size and leakage trade off.

\section{Pooled or per-episode: where the model is fitted}

The M-step decides between our objective and SMiRL. A model $q$ fitted once, across all episodes, gives $-H[p_\pi]$, the pooled objective. A model refitted within each episode $e$, as in SMiRL \citep{Berseth-smirl21}, gives $-\E_e H[\hat p_e]$ for the episode's own outcome law $\hat p_e$. With $\bar p = \E_e[\hat p_e]$, concavity gives
\begin{equation}
  H(\bar p) - \E_e H(\hat p_e) = I(Z;\, E) \ge 0,
\end{equation}
checked numerically. So the per-episode ELBO exceeds the pooled one by exactly the information the episode carries about the outcome, the $I(Z;\text{trial})$ term of \citet{Mutti-convex23}. The same identity applies to a posterior over policies, with the policy in place of the episode.

\section{Working backwards from a regularised objective}

The sections above start from an event and derive the objective. The converse starts from an objective and reads off the event. Any objective in Gibbs form is an ELBO,
\begin{equation}
  \E_Q[f] - \mathrm{KL}(Q\,\|\,P) = \ln Z - \mathrm{KL}\big(Q \,\big\|\, P e^{f}/Z\big), \qquad Z = \E_P e^{f},
\end{equation}
so the prior is $P$, the likelihood $e^f$, the evidence $Z$ and the posterior $P e^f/Z$. Which KL regularises the objective decides what the posterior is over.

\paragraph{Occupancy KL.} Take
\begin{equation}
  \min_\pi\; H[\dpi K] + \lambda\, \mathrm{KL}(\dpi \,\|\, \mu), \qquad \mu \text{ a law on } \mathcal S\times\mathcal A .
  \label{eq:occkl}
\end{equation}
With $H[p] = \min_q \E_p[-\ln q]$, for fixed $q$ and any law $d$ on state-action pairs, the KL is an expectation under $d$ and joins the cross-entropy under one expectation over $(s,a,z) \sim dK$:
\begin{equation}
  \E_{dK}\big[-\ln q(z)\big] + \lambda\, \E_d\Big[\ln \frac{d(s,a)}{\mu(s,a)}\Big]
  = \lambda\, \E_{dK}\Big[ \ln \frac{d(s,a)\,K(z\mid s,a)}{\mu(s,a)\,K(z\mid s,a)\,q(z)^{1/\lambda}} \Big]
  = \lambda\Big( \mathrm{KL}\big(dK \,\big\|\, \Pi_q\big) - \ln Z_q \Big),
  \label{eq:jointkl}
\end{equation}
where $K$ has been multiplied in above and below, and
\begin{equation}
  \Pi_q(s,a,z) = \frac{\mu(s,a)\,K(z\mid s,a)\,q(z)^{1/\lambda}}{Z_q}, \qquad Z_q = \sum_{s,a,z} \mu(s,a)\,K(z\mid s,a)\,q(z)^{1/\lambda}.
\end{equation}
Read backwards, the prior is $\mu K$ over state, action and outcome, the likelihood is exactly the prediction event $q(z)^{1/\lambda}$ of Section~1, and the posterior is $\Pi_q$. At $\lambda=1$ the evidence $Z_q = P_{\mu K}(O=1)$ is the probability that a pair drawn from the reference has its outcome guessed, for any channel. Nothing about the event is assumed; it is what the objective says.

\paragraph{The channel is held fixed.} The variational family in \eqref{eq:jointkl} is $dK$: the regulator chooses $d$, and nature keeps $K$. Minimising over unconstrained $d$ gives
\begin{equation}
  d^\star_q(s,a) \propto \mu(s,a)\, e^{r_q(s,a)/\lambda}, \qquad r_q(s,a) = \sum_z K(z\mid s,a)\ln q(z),
\end{equation}
not the $(s,a)$-marginal $\mu(s,a)\sum_z K(z\mid s,a)\, q(z)^{1/\lambda}$ of $\Pi_q$. The two differ by Jensen, $e^{\sum_z K \ln q} \le \sum_z K q$, and agree for a deterministic channel. So the geometric-mean form of the reward is the price of not letting the posterior pick the channel's outcome, the same fix that removes optimism over the dynamics.

\paragraph{The policy is an I-projection.} The posterior $d^\star_q$ is in general not an occupancy. The policy's occupancy is its I-projection onto the occupancy polytope, $\arg\min_{d\in\Dcal}\mathrm{KL}(d\,\|\,d^\star_q)$. With multipliers $V$ for the Bellman flow constraints, stationarity gives
\begin{equation}
  d_\pi(s,a) \propto \mu(s,a)\, \exp\!\Big( \tfrac1\lambda \big( r_q(s,a) + \gamma \textstyle\sum_{s'} P(s'\mid s,a) V(s') - V(s) \big) \Big),
\end{equation}
with $V$ minimising the convex dual $(1-\gamma)\,\nu^\top V + \lambda \sum_{s,a} \mu(s,a)\, e^{\delta_V(s,a)/\lambda - 1}$, where $\delta_V$ is the bracket above. This is the form of relative entropy policy search \citep{Peters-relative10}. So \eqref{eq:occkl} is REPS on the prediction event, with $q$ refitted by maximum likelihood between projections.

\paragraph{Policy KL instead.} The trajectory KL of Section~1 keeps the dynamics fixed, and only the policy term survives: soft Bellman backups and a posterior over trajectories. The two regularisers differ by the state marginal,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{KL}(\dpi \,\|\, \mu) = \E_{s\sim \dpi} \mathrm{KL}\big(\pi(\cdot\mid s)\,\|\,\mu(\cdot\mid s)\big) + \mathrm{KL}(d_S \,\|\, \mu_S).
\end{equation}
Both entropy regularisers, joint and conditional, are unified by \citet{Neu-unified17}; the state-marginal term alone is state-marginal matching \citep{Lee-efficient19}.

\paragraph{What the state-marginal term does.} Take $\mu$ uniform on $n$ state-action pairs and a deterministic channel. Then $H(S,A) = H(Z) + H(S,A\mid Z)$, and
\begin{equation}
  H[\dpi K] + \lambda\,\mathrm{KL}(\dpi\,\|\,\mu) = (1-\lambda)\, H(Z) - \lambda\, H(S,A\mid Z) + \lambda \ln n .
\end{equation}
For $\lambda < 1$ the outcome still concentrates, and among the pairs that give the same outcome the policy spreads as widely as it can: predictable in what matters, free in what does not. At $\lambda \ge 1$ the outcome no longer concentrates. For $\lambda > 0$ the objective is concave minus convex, so the deterministic vertex is lost, and it returns as $\lambda \to 0$. The within-level-set spread links to the maximum-occupancy principle \citep{Ramirez-Ruiz-complex22} and to action-state entropy regularisation \citep{Grytskyy-general23}.

\paragraph{Checks.} On a random MDP with 4 states, 3 actions, $\gamma = 0.9$ and $\lambda = 0.5$, the REPS form from the dual satisfies the flow constraints to $3\times10^{-8}$ and matches the objective of direct policy search to $10^{-7}$. The joint-KL form \eqref{eq:jointkl} and the level-set identity hold to machine precision. Script: \texttt{notes\_joery/scripts/reps\_check.py}, run with \texttt{uv run --with numpy --with scipy}.

\open{Which regulariser for the paper: the policy KL, with a posterior over trajectories and a deterministic optimum in the limit; or the occupancy KL, with a posterior over state-action pairs, REPS, and spread within the outcome's level set?}

\section{Neighbours to check before anything is written}

From the survey knowledge base, node \texttt{S5/convex-twins/exponential-kl-inference}, coded opposite sign:
\begin{itemize}
\item The duality $-\lambda \ln \E_P e^{-J/\lambda} = \inf_Q \E_Q J + \lambda\,\mathrm{KL}(Q\|P)$ in path-integral control \citep{Theodorou-relative12,Kappen-path05} is the Gibbs principle used here, with a linear cost.
\item VAPOR \citep{Tarbouriech-probabilistic23} maximises $\lambda^\top \E r + H_\tau(\lambda)$, an occupancy entropy with the opposite sign, and shows the mean Thompson occupancy is a mixture of deterministic vertices.
\item \citet{Milosevic-dually26} give the geometry of planning as inference; \citet{Milosevic-active26} pose active inference as a convex MDP, the convex mirror.
\item \citet{ODonoghue-making20}: the probability-of-optimality surrogate ignores epistemic uncertainty and fails on DeepSea. Relevant to whether the event should sit on the belief MDP.
\item Variational policy gradient for general utilities \citep{Zhang-variational20}.
\item Gibbs posteriors in generalised Bayes, Bissiri, Holmes and Walker 2016, not yet in the bibliography.
\end{itemize}
