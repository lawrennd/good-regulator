\section[Identifiability through empowerment]{Identifiability through empowerment}
\label{sec:pomdp}
Suppose we turn the recipe from \S\ref{sec:objective} directly into an algorithm.
We take the entropy criterion of Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}, with its coarse-graining steps supplying both the reward and the modelling conditions.
To see what such an algorithm must estimate, we rewrite the criterion through Gibbs' inequality as
\begin{equation} \label{eq:gibbs_criterion}
  \min_\pi H_\nu(\psi_\#\occ) = \min_\pi \min_q -\E_{Z \sim \psi_\#\occ}\big[\ln \tfrac{\dd q}{\dd\nu}(Z)\big],
\end{equation}
and see that it splits into an outer and an inner minimisation, where the inner minimum is attained at $q = \psi_\#\occ$.
So, as with the tangent in the convex RL machinery of \S\ref{sec:prelim}, a fixed $q$ makes the criterion linear and reduces the full optimisation to an iteration of conventional RL.
In other words, the outer problem with $q$ held fixed is RL with reward $\ln \tfrac{\dd q}{\dd\nu}(Z)$ and the inner problem is to estimate the outcome law of the current policy from finite data.
In general, an algorithm must therefore learn the model components $\hat b$, $\hat\psi$, $\iota$ and $\hat P$ that the modelling conditions impose, along with an estimate $\hat q$ of the regulator's own outcome law on $\Ycal$.
As every term of Eq.~\eqref{eq:classes} is a functional of the $Y$ law, $\hat q$ is linked to the outcome space $\Zcal$ through $\iota$.
In theory $\hat q$ is determined by the model and the policy, so a separate estimate only amortises internal computation.
This amortisation is still part of the learner's affordances under Definition~\ref{def:affordances}, since under bounded reasoning $\hat q$ is bottlenecked by its class and capacity.
This structure and imposed modelling conditions serve as the main algorithm principles.

However, plugging these estimates naively into the iteration for Eq.~\eqref{eq:gibbs_criterion} introduces the dark room problem \citep{Friston-dark12}.
This means that the policy moves to a stable state where nothing happens, or the learner collapses the representation into a `dark' stable state.
The first cause was a criterion that is indifferent among such stable states, which \S\ref{sec:objective} already resolved through the goal measure $\nu$ that reassigns volume to the desired outcomes.
The second cause and the focus of this section is identifiability.
This is a typical RL pathology of finite data at both levels, since the policy and the learner minimise the same objective that is unobserved past the policy's data.
This section addresses it through empowerment at each level, this entails a mutual information (MI) term that the entropy criterion of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gibbs_criterion} minimises by default.
We bound the empowerment terms at a floor such that the regulator keeps an exploration budget until its models are grounded and an optimal policy is found.
We connect the empowerment terms to Bayesian exploration and active perception.

\paragraph{Identifiability and the MI-constrained program.}
Assume the iteration of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gibbs_criterion} has converged to a fixed point $(\pi^\star, q^\star)$, where $\pi^\star$ minimises the entropy of the outcome law $q^\star$ that its endogenous cause-control model predicts.
To define identifiability, we ask which environments are consistent with this model under $\pi^\star$, which means that they produce the same outcome law $q^\star$ under the policy.
Such environments obtain equal outcome entropy and are thus indistinguishable to the criterion.
\begin{definition}[Confusing and consistent set]
\label{def:confusing}
    Let $d^{\pi}_\theta$ be the occupancy of $\pi$ in a (reward-free) POMDP $\theta$ as defined by $(P, \omega, \rho_0)$ of \S\ref{sec:prelim}.
    The consistent set of a fixed point $(\pi^\star, q^\star)$ is $\Theta(\pi^\star) = \{ \theta : \psi_\# d^{\pi^\star}_\theta = q^\star \}$, and its confusing set is $\Theta_c(\pi^\star) = \big\{ \theta \in \Theta(\pi^\star) \;:\; H_\nu(q^\star) > \min_{\pi} H_\nu(\psi_\# d^{\pi}_\theta) \big\}.$
\end{definition}
We say that the solution to our good regulator recipe is optimal when a fixed point $(\pi^\star, q^\star)$ satisfies $\Theta_c(\pi^\star) = \emptyset$.
Any other fixed point with a non-empty $\Theta_c$ sits on a dark-room spectrum.
Due to the coarse-graining of $\hat q$, $\Theta(\pi^\star)$ need not be a singleton, so an empty $\Theta_c$ identifies the environment only up to equivalence under the criterion similar to bisimulation methods \citep{Givan-equivalence03}.


We now structure the entropy criterion of Eq.~\eqref{eq:classes} over the variables that generate an outcome, so that we can relate these to the confusing set.
Since the regulator never perfectly observes the environment $\theta$ or its state $S$ in a POMDP, our structure follows from the error-control to cause-control insight from \S\ref{sec:objective} to impose the model structure.
The variable that generate an outcome are exactly the regulator's affordances under Def.~\ref{def:affordances}.
For the policy these follow from the history $X$ and its action $A$, which together generate the prediction $Y$ with law $\hat q$.
For the learner these are its criterion-relevant estimates, where the approximate belief $\hat b$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} gives the law over both a latent state $\hat S$ and a hypothesis $\hat\theta$ over the components $\hat P$, $\hat\psi$ and $\iota$, which track $S$ and $\theta$.
These items only need to satisfy the criterion such that $\hat\theta$ need not identify the full environment $(P, \omega, \rho_0)$.
To structure the criterion over these variables we need a regularity condition, this is stated by the assumption below.
\begin{assumption}[Finite resolution]
    \label{ass:kernel}
    The prediction $Y$ given $(X,A)$ has a density against $\rho$ and the outcome $Z$ given $(\hat S, \hat\theta, A)$ has a density against $\lambda$, both with finite entropy.
\end{assumption}
Thus, the regulator must predict at a finite resolution to prevent infinite entropies for deterministic predictions on continuous $\Ycal$.
The chain rule of MI over all variables gives the following proposition.
\begin{proposition}[Entropy criterion decomposition]
    \label{prop:split-pomdp}
    The predictive and residual of Eq.~\eqref{eq:classes} split as
    \begin{equation} \label{eq:split-pomdp}
    \setlength{\belowdisplayskip}{0pt}
    \begin{aligned}
      H_\rho(Y) &= I(X;Y) + I_A(A;Y \mid X) + H_\rho(Y \mid X,A), \\
      H_\lambda(Z \mid Y) &= I_Y(X,A;Z \mid Y) + I_{\hat\theta}(\hat\theta;Z \mid X,A) + I_{\hat S}(\hat S;Z \mid X,A,\hat\theta) + H_\lambda(Z \mid \hat S,\hat\theta,A).
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}
\end{proposition}
Proposition~\ref{prop:split-pomdp} splits the criterion over the policy (top) and the learner (bottom) regulators.
To digest the terms, the policy splits into a regulation term $I(X;Y)$, an empowerment term $I_A$ \citep{Klyubin-all05}, and the irreducible noise of the coarse-graining $H_\rho(Y \mid X,A)$.
The empowerment relates to an endogenous action-collapse split if we observe $I_A = H(A \mid X) - H(A \mid X, Y)$, where the first term is the policy stochasticity and the second is the entropy of the posterior policy $p(A \mid X, Y)$ conditioned on the outcome $Y$.
This latter term specifically relates to inverse dynamics of sensorimotor world models \citep{Ivashkov-smwm26} and soft-optimal policies from control as inference \citep[see \S\ref{sec:related},][]{Levine-reinforcement18}.
The learner splits equivalently over $\Zcal$ rather than $\Ycal$, into a regulation term $I_Y$ that compresses the outcome prediction from the history, the empowerment terms $I_{\hat\theta}$ and $I_{\hat S}$, and the irreducible noise $H_\lambda(Z \mid \hat S,\hat\theta,A)$.
The irreducible noise on the learner level is the actual irreducible entropy floor of the world.
The learner-empowerment terms connect to the information gain of Bayesian exploration \citep{MacKay-informationbased92} for $I_{\hat\theta}$ and active perception \citep{Bajcsy-active88} for $I_{\hat S}$.
The combined MI gives $I_{\hat\theta} + I_{\hat S} = \E_{\occ}\big[\KL\big(p(\hat\theta,\hat S \mid X,A,Z) \,\|\, p(\hat\theta,\hat S \mid X,A)\big)\big]$, which describes how much an observed outcome is expected to move the belief $\hat b(\hat \theta, \hat S)$.
It is precisely this mechanism, linked to the outcome law on $\Zcal$, that shrinks the confusing set of Definition~\ref{def:confusing}.
However, as mentioned before, the current criterion drives all terms of Proposition~\ref{prop:split-pomdp} down to an irreducible floor.


We therefore impose the empowerment terms of Eq.~\eqref{eq:split-pomdp} as explicit constraints on the criterion, $\min_\pi H_\nu(\psi_\#\occ)$ subject to a floor $I_i \ge c_i, i \in \{A, \hat\theta, \hat S\}$.
For $\mu_i \ge 0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:gibbs_criterion} admits the Lagrangian
\begin{equation} \label{eq:lagrangian}
  \min_\pi \min_q \max_{\mu \ge 0} \; -\E_{Z \sim \psi_\#\occ}\big[\ln \tfrac{\dd q}{\dd\nu}(Z)\big] - \sum\nolimits_{i} \mu_i \big(I_i - c_i\big).
\end{equation}
This again reuses the convex RL machinery of \S\ref{sec:prelim} by holding $q$ and $\mu$ fixed in the outer problem to obtain a conventional RL problem.
The information terms act as bonuses weighted by $\mu$ next to the reward $\ln \tfrac{\dd q}{\dd\nu}(Z)$, which preserve exploration and policy stochasticity at the cost of increased entropy.
They are also similar to the expected free energy \citep{Friston-active15}, which uses fixed weights for the learner bonuses, whereas ours are a constraint derived from the criterion.
The final steps are then choosing $c_i$ so that we converge to an optimal regulator, and an algorithm for Eq.~\eqref{eq:lagrangian}.

\begin{proposition}[Information budget]
\label{prop:budget}
\add{Let the regulator observe only the outcome, $X_{t+1} = (X_t, A_t, Z_t)$, with an exact Bayes belief over $(\hat\theta, \hat S_t)$, and let the run end at a time $\tau$ of finite mean decided by the history and independent randomness.}
\add{Under the occupancy of the run, every policy satisfies}

  \E[\tau]\, \big(I_{\hat\theta} + I_{\hat S}\big) \;\ge\; \E[\tau]\, I(W; Z \mid X, A) \;=\; D_0 - D_\tau + \E[\tau]\, \bar\Delta,
