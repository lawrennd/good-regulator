\section{Measure-theoretic primer}
\label{sup:primer}
Measure theory generalises the notion of counting to volume over mathematical spaces \citep{Billingsley-probability12}.
We use it to express the ideas of the good regulator \citep{Conant-good70} in reinforcement learning terms, and to manipulate entropy, divergences and mutual information as functionals of the laws of random variables.
This section is a brief self-contained primer on the key concepts, since we expect an RL audience to be familiar with probability theory but less so with measure theory.

\paragraph{\add{Measures.}}
\add{A $\sigma$-algebra on $\Zcal$ is a collection of measurable subsets that contains $\Zcal$ and is closed under complements and countable unions, here all subsets of a countable $\Zcal$ or the Borel sets of $\mathbb R^n$ generated by the open sets.}
\add{A measure $m$ assigns each measurable $B$ a volume $m(B) \in [0, \infty]$, with $m(\emptyset) = 0$ and additively over countably many disjoint sets, and a law is a measure with $m(\Zcal) = 1$.}
\add{Our two bases, the counting measure on a countable $\Zcal$ and the Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb R^n$, are $\sigma$-finite, so $\Zcal$ is a countable union of sets of finite volume, and turn $\int g\,\dd m$ into $\sum_z g(z)$ and $\int g(z)\,\dd z$.}
\add{A property holds almost everywhere under $m$ if it fails only on a set of $m$-volume zero, and $\nu$ is absolutely continuous with respect to $\lambda$, $\nu \ll \lambda$, if every set of $\lambda$-volume zero has $\nu$-volume zero.}
\add{For a $\sigma$-finite $\lambda$ and a law $\nu \ll \lambda$ the Radon--Nikodym theorem gives a density $\tfrac{\dd\nu}{\dd\lambda} \ge 0$, unique almost everywhere under $\lambda$, with $\int g\,\dd\nu = \int g\,\tfrac{\dd\nu}{\dd\lambda}\,\dd\lambda$.}
\add{Along a chain $p \ll q \ll m$ densities multiply, $\tfrac{\dd p}{\dd m} = \tfrac{\dd p}{\dd q}\,\tfrac{\dd q}{\dd m}$, so $\tfrac{\dd p}{\dd q} = \tfrac{\dd p}{\dd m} / \tfrac{\dd q}{\dd m}$ almost everywhere under $p$.}

\paragraph{\add{Maps and products.}}
\add{A map $\psi\colon \mathcal X \to \Zcal$ is measurable if pre-images $\psi^{-1}(B)$ of measurable sets are measurable, as we assume of all maps and functions, and it pulls $g$ on $\Zcal$ back to $g \circ \psi$ and pushes $m$ on $\mathcal X$ forward to $\psi_\# m(B) = m(\psi^{-1}(B))$, so $\int g\,\dd(\psi_\# m) = \int g \circ \psi\,\dd m$.}
\add{Two $\sigma$-finite measures $m$ on $\mathcal X$ and $m'$ on $\Zcal$ give the product measure $m \otimes m'$ on $\mathcal X \times \Zcal$ with the $\sigma$-algebra generated by rectangles, the unique measure with ${(m \otimes m')(C \times B) = m(C)\, m'(B)}$.}
\add{A law $p$ on $\mathcal X \times \Zcal$ has as marginals its pushforwards $p_{\mathcal X}$ and $p_{\Zcal}$ under the two projections, so $p_{\mathcal X}(C) = p(C \times \Zcal)$, and $p_{\mathcal X} \otimes p_{\Zcal}$ keeps these marginals but forgets how the coordinates vary together.}

\paragraph{\add{Kernels.}}
\add{A kernel $k$ from $\mathcal X$ to $\Zcal$ is a law $k(\cdot \mid x)$ on $\Zcal$ for each $x$, measurable in $x$, and with a law $p_{\mathcal X}$ it builds the joint law $p(C \times B) = \int_C k(B \mid x)\,\dd p_{\mathcal X}(x)$, the measure form of $p(x,z) = p(x)\, k(z \mid x)$.}
\add{On standard Borel spaces, which include countable sets, $\mathbb R^n$ and their finite products, every joint law disintegrates into $p_{\mathcal X}$ and a conditional kernel $p(\cdot \mid x)$, unique for almost every $x$ under $p_{\mathcal X}$, and transition laws, policies and outcome channels are all kernels.}

\paragraph{\add{Entropy.}}
\add{The entropy of a law $p \ll m$ against a base $m$ is $H_m(p) = -\int \ln \tfrac{\dd p}{\dd m}\,\dd p = -\int \tfrac{\dd p}{\dd m} \ln \tfrac{\dd p}{\dd m}\,\dd m$, the Shannon entropy under counting $m$, where the density is the mass $p(\{z\})$, and the differential entropy under Lebesgue $m$.}
\add{It may be infinite, and every identity below is read where the entropies involved are finite.}
\add{Since $\tfrac{\dd p}{\dd p} = 1$ we have $H_p(p) = 0$, and for $p \ll q \ll m$ the quotient of densities gives the change of base $H_q(p) = H_m(p) + \E_p[\ln \tfrac{\dd q}{\dd m}]$.}
\add{For a law $q$ this is the divergence $H_q(p) = -\KL(p \,\|\, q)$, and Gibbs' inequality $\KL(p \,\|\, q) \ge 0$ with equality only at $q = p$ gives $H_m(p) = \min_q \E_p[-\ln \tfrac{\dd q}{\dd m}]$ over laws $q \ll m$, with $-\ln 0 = +\infty$.}
\add{As a minimum of functionals linear in $p$, $H_m$ is concave where finite.}

\paragraph{\add{Conditional entropy.}}
\add{For a joint law $p$ with $p(\cdot \mid x) \ll m'$ for almost every $x$ under $p_{\mathcal X}$, the conditional entropy $H_{m'}(p \mid \mathcal X) = \int H_{m'}(p(\cdot \mid x))\,\dd p_{\mathcal X}(x)$ averages the entropy of the kernel, so only the conditioned coordinate carries a base.}
\add{A point-mass kernel, as for a function $z = \psi(x)$, has zero conditional entropy against the counting measure and none against the Lebesgue measure, to which it has no density.}
\add{For $p \ll m \otimes m'$ the density factors almost everywhere as $\tfrac{\dd p}{\dd (m \otimes m')}(x,z) = \tfrac{\dd p_{\mathcal X}}{\dd m}(x)\, \tfrac{\dd p(\cdot \mid x)}{\dd m'}(z)$, and taking $-\E_p \ln$ gives the chain rule $H_{m \otimes m'}(p) = H_m(p_{\mathcal X}) + H_{m'}(p \mid \mathcal X)$, or $H_{m'}(p_{\Zcal}) + H_m(p \mid \Zcal)$ along the other coordinate.}

\paragraph{\add{Mutual information.}}
\add{Mutual information is the negative entropy against the product of the marginals, $I(p) = -H_{p_{\mathcal X} \otimes p_{\Zcal}}(p) = \KL(p \,\|\, p_{\mathcal X} \otimes p_{\Zcal})$, set to $+\infty$ when $p$ is not absolutely continuous to that product, so it depends on which coordinates are separated but on no base.}
\add{For $p \ll m \otimes m'$ the density of $p_{\mathcal X} \otimes p_{\Zcal}$ against $m \otimes m'$ is the product of the marginal densities, so the change of base gives $I(p) = H_m(p_{\mathcal X}) + H_{m'}(p_{\Zcal}) - H_{m \otimes m'}(p)$, and the chain rule gives $I(p) = H_{m'}(p_{\Zcal}) - H_{m'}(p \mid \mathcal X) = H_m(p_{\mathcal X}) - H_m(p \mid \Zcal)$.}

\paragraph{\add{Random variables.}}
\add{A random variable $Z$ is a measurable map from a probability space $(\Omega, \mathcal F, \Pr)$ into $\Zcal$ with law $Z_\# \Pr$, a pair $(X,Z)$ has a joint law on $\mathcal X \times \Zcal$ whose marginals are the laws of $X$ and $Z$, and $Z = \psi(S,A)$ has the pushforward under $\psi$ of the joint law of $(S,A)$.}
\add{We write $H_m(Z)$, $H_{m'}(Z \mid X)$ and $I(X;Z)$ for the functionals above on the joint law of the named variables, each entropy carrying the base of the variable left of the bar, so the chain rules read $H_{m \otimes m'}(X,Z) = H_m(X) + H_{m'}(Z \mid X)$ and $I(X;Z) = H_{m'}(Z) - H_{m'}(Z \mid X)$.}
\add{The conditional $I(X;Z \mid Y)$ averages the mutual information of the joint law of $(X,Z)$ given $Y = y$ under the law of $Y$, with $I(X;Z,Y) = I(X;Y) + I(X;Z \mid Y)$.}

\section{Models as $\sigma$-algebras}
\label{sup:sigma-models}
\begingroup\color{red}
Conant and Ashby show that the simplest optimal regulator is a mapping $h$ from the states of the system to actions, and call such a regulator a model of the system.
A cause-controlled regulator that acts on the disturbance $D$ realises this mapping only as $h \circ \phi$ through the relation $\phi\colon D \to S$, so it must also model how the disturbance causes the state.
This section restates that notion as a $\sigma$-algebra, which makes precise the two coarse-grainings behind Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}, the resolution at which the regulator reads its history and the resolution at which the outcome is measured, and shows that both are set by the regulator's actions, the first by what the optimal action and its update need from the history and the second by what the pairs can change in the outcome.
The minimal model resolves the history only as far as its optimal action and its own update require, and the outcome needs resolving no further than the distinctions some pair can change.
What no pair changes enters the criterion only through the volume the goal measure gives each cell, so a reward $-\ln \tfrac{\dd\nu}{\dd\lambda}$ carries a discretisation choice next to a preference.

All variables live on one probability space, that of the process under the policy, with $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal F$.
For a variable $Y$, $\sigma(Y) = \{Y^{-1}(C) : C \text{ measurable}\} \subseteq \mathcal F$ is the sub-$\sigma$-algebra it generates, the coarsest that makes $Y$ measurable, which holds the events that $Y$ can tell apart.
For a discrete $Y$ it is generated by the partition of the space into the fibres $\{Y = y\}$, so $\sigma(Y') \subseteq \sigma(Y)$ says that every cell of $Y'$ is a union of cells of $Y$, and in the discrete case a smaller $\sigma$-algebra is a coarser partition of the same space.
By the Doob--Dynkin lemma $Y' = f(Y)$ for a measurable $f$ if and only if $\sigma(Y') \subseteq \sigma(Y)$ \citep{Billingsley-probability12}.
For a fully observed system, a deterministic policy is thus an action with $\sigma(A) \subseteq \sigma(S)$, which is the mapping $h\colon S \to R$ of \citet{Conant-good70} and is equivalent to $H(A \mid S) = 0$ against counting.
Inclusions between $\sigma$-algebras below hold up to null sets.
Throughout, $\Zcal$ is standard Borel, every $\kappa(s,a) \ll \lambda$, and the entropies that appear are finite.

\begin{definition}[Model]
\label{def:sigma-model}
A model is the $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal M = \sigma(M) \subseteq \sigma(X)$ of a statistic $M$ of the history, with values in a standard Borel space of its own, such that the policy $\pi(a \mid x)$ depends on $x$ only through $M(x)$.
Writing $M_t = M(X_t)$, it is closed if $M_{t+1} = u(M_t, A_t, O_{t+1})$ for a measurable $u$, equivalently $\sigma(M_{t+1}) \subseteq \sigma(M_t, A_t, O_{t+1})$.
\end{definition}
\noindent The definition fixes what the regulator resolves and not where $M$ is kept, so a statistic it reads off the world, such as a thermometer, is a model in the same sense as one it stores.
Consistency of Section~\ref{sec:objective} holds by construction, since the model predicts the conditional law $\Pr(Z \in \cdot \mid M, A)$.
The belief $b(X)$ is a closed model for every policy that reads the history through it, and it splits into the posterior $M_\theta$ over the law $\theta = (P,\kappa)$ and the filter $M_S$ over the state given $\theta$, so that $\sigma(b(X)) = \sigma(M_\theta) \vee \sigma(M_S)$.
The terms $\bar I_P + \bar I_\kappa$ and $\bar I_S$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:split-pomdp} are what these two components still have to resolve about the class.
Closure is where the second regulator of Section~\ref{sec:pomdp} acts, with the belief $M_t$ as its state, the update $u$ as its policy and the drift in $\theta$ as its disturbance.
A posterior over a drifting $\theta_t$ is closed only if $u$ carries the law of the drift, so a learner that regulates against drift is error-controlled one level up and must model how the drift moves the law and not only the law.
We state this as a reading, since Section~\ref{sec:pomdp} gives the second regulator in prose.

\begin{proposition}[Resolution of a model]
\label{prop:sigma-model}
Let $\mathcal M$ be a model, $\hat Z$ the observable class of \del{Definition~1}\add{Section~\ref{sec:objective}} and $\tilde Z$ the true class of Section~\ref{sec:pomdp}.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The observable class is the true class projected onto the history and the action, $\hat Z = \E[\tilde Z \mid X, A]$, which is the definition of $\hat\kappa$ read under the prior.
  \item The model predicts the outcome law as well as the history does, $\Pr(Z \in \cdot \mid M, A) = \hat Z$, if and only if $\sigma(\hat Z) \subseteq \mathcal M \vee \sigma(A)$, and in general $H_\lambda(Z \mid M, A) = H_\lambda(Z \mid \hat Z) + I(Z;\hat Z \mid M, A)$.
  \item The belief always suffices, $\sigma(\hat Z) \subseteq \sigma(b(X), A)$.
  \item $Z$ is independent of $(S,A,\theta)$ given $\tilde Z$, and every $\mathcal C \subseteq \sigma(S,A,\theta)$ with this property contains $\sigma(\tilde Z)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Part 1.}
The outcome depends on the history only through $(S,A,\theta)$ and the policy depends on $(S,\theta)$ only through the history, so $\Pr(Z \in B \mid X, A) = \E[\kappa(S,A)(B) \mid X, A]$ for every measurable $B$.
\emph{Part 2.}
Since $\mathcal M \vee \sigma(A) \subseteq \sigma(X,A)$, the tower property gives $\Pr(Z \in B \mid M, A) = \E[\hat Z(B) \mid \mathcal M \vee \sigma(A)]$, which equals $\hat Z(B)$ if and only if $\hat Z(B)$ is $\mathcal M \vee \sigma(A)$-measurable, and countably many $B$ determine $\hat Z$ on a standard Borel $\Zcal$.
Since $\Pr(Z \in B \mid X,A) = \hat Z(B)$ is $\sigma(\hat Z)$-measurable, it equals $\Pr(Z \in B \mid \hat Z)$, so $Z$ is independent of $(X,A)$ and hence of $(M,A)$ given $\hat Z$, which gives $H_\lambda(Z \mid M,A,\hat Z) = H_\lambda(Z \mid \hat Z)$, and the chain rule gives the identity.
Its last term is zero if and only if $\Pr(Z \in B \mid M,A) = \Pr(Z \in B \mid M,A,\hat Z) = \hat Z(B)$ for every $B$, by the independence just shown, which is the first statement.
\emph{Part 3.}
$\hat\kappa(x,a) = \E[\kappa(S,a) \mid X = x]$ integrates against the posterior over $(S,\theta)$, which is the belief $b(x)$, and Doob--Dynkin gives the inclusion.
\emph{Part 4.}
$\Pr(Z \in \cdot \mid S,A,\theta) = \kappa(S,A) = \tilde Z$ is $\sigma(\tilde Z)$-measurable, which gives the independence, and for any such $\mathcal C$ it equals $\Pr(Z \in \cdot \mid \mathcal C)$, which is $\mathcal C$-measurable.
\end{proof}
\noindent A model with $\sigma(\hat Z) \not\subseteq \mathcal M \vee \sigma(A)$ thus merges classes, and since $Z$ given $\Pr(Z \in \cdot \mid M,A)$ is drawn from that law, Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} holds with it in place of $\hat Z$, with the merged information $I(Z;\hat Z \mid M,A)$ moved from control to noise and the criterion value unchanged.
The minimal model of Section~\ref{sec:objective} is the coarsest closed $\mathcal M \subseteq \sigma(b(X))$ that carries a minimiser of \eqref{eq:classes}, and $\mathcal M \vee \sigma(A)$ need not contain $\sigma(\hat Z)$ where merged classes share an optimal action.
Part 4 is the $\sigma$-algebra form of the sufficiency of $\tilde Z$ used in Section~\ref{sec:pomdp}, where it makes the noise term the channel noise alone.

The second coarse-graining is of the outcome.
A regulator that measures $Z$ only up to a countable partition $\{B_j\}$ of $\Zcal$ sees the cell $J$ with $Z \in B_J$, and the base measure decides which cells count as equal.

\begin{proposition}[Coarse-graining as a change of measure]
\label{prop:coarse-grain}
Let $p \ll \nu$ be the law of $Z$, let every cell have $0 < \nu(B_j) < \infty$, let $H_\nu(Z)$ be finite, let the partition be finite or $\E[\KL(p(\cdot \mid J) \,\|\, \bar\nu_J)]$ be finite, and let $\bar\nu_j = \nu(\cdot \cap B_j) / \nu(B_j)$.
Then
\begin{equation}
  H_\nu(Z) = H_{J_\# \nu}(J) - \E\big[\KL\big(p(\cdot \mid J) \,\|\, \bar\nu_J\big)\big],
  \label{eq:coarse-grain}
\end{equation}
so coarse-graining never lowers the entropy and keeps it exactly when $Z$ is distributed as $\nu$ within each cell.
For cells of equal volume $\nu(B_j) = \Delta$ this reads $H_\nu(Z) = H(J) + \ln \Delta - \E[\KL(p(\cdot \mid J) \,\|\, \bar\nu_J)]$ with $H(J)$ against counting.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On $B_j$ the density factors as $\tfrac{\dd p}{\dd \nu} = \tfrac{p(B_j)}{\nu(B_j)}\, \tfrac{\dd p(\cdot \mid j)}{\dd \bar\nu_j}$, and taking $-\E_p \ln$ gives \eqref{eq:coarse-grain}, since $J_\# \nu$ has mass $\nu(B_j)$ on $j$.
For equal cells $J_\# \nu = \Delta$ times counting, and the change of base gives $H_{J_\# \nu}(J) = H(J) + \ln \Delta$.
\end{proof}
\noindent A counting entropy of a coarse-grained outcome is thus an entropy against the measure under which its cells are equal, up to the within-cell divergence and the constant $\ln\Delta$.
Choosing $\nu$ is choosing which coarse-graining counts as uniform, so a reward $r = -\ln \tfrac{\dd\nu}{\dd\lambda}$ carries a choice of which outcomes count as equal in volume next to a preference over them.

If no pair changes how the outcome is spread within a cell, the regulator affects no distinction finer than the cells, and the detail below them can be folded into the measure.

\begin{corollary}[Coarse-graining to what the regulator can act on]
\label{cor:affordance}
Under the conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:coarse-grain}, let every cell have a law $q_j$ such that $\kappa(s,a)(\cdot \mid B_j) = q_j$ for every pair with $\kappa(s,a)(B_j) > 0$, and let $c_j = \KL(q_j \,\|\, \bar\nu_j)$ be finite.
Then for every policy
\begin{equation}
  H_\nu(Z) = H_{\nu'}(J), \qquad \nu'(j) = \nu(B_j)\, e^{-c_j},
  \label{eq:affordance}
\end{equation}
so the criterion on the outcome and the criterion on its cells under $\nu'$ have the same minimisers.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The law of $Z$ given $J = j$ mixes the laws $\kappa(s,a)(\cdot \mid B_j)$ over the visited pairs, which all equal $q_j$, so $p(\cdot \mid j) = q_j$ for every policy and the last term of \eqref{eq:coarse-grain} is $\E[c_J]$.
The change of base from $J_\# \nu$ to $\nu'$ gives $H_{\nu'}(J) = H_{J_\# \nu}(J) - \E[c_J]$, which is \eqref{eq:coarse-grain}.
\end{proof}
\noindent A regulator may therefore coarse-grain the outcome to the level at which its actions make a difference without changing its problem, and the detail it cannot act on survives only as the volume $\nu'$ gives each cell.
Where the cells are chosen coarser than that, the within-cell laws differ across pairs and the divergence term of \eqref{eq:coarse-grain} in general depends on the policy, so no single $\nu'$ absorbs it and the coarse criterion can select different minimisers.
\endgroup

\section{Proofs}
\label{sup:proofs}

Throughout, $\lambda$ is the base measure on $\Zcal$, all laws are absolutely continuous with respect to the references they are measured against, and conditional entropies are averaged over the conditioning variable.

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}}
\label{sup:proof-criterion}
First apply the change of base for $\nu \ll \lambda$, which gives $H_\nu(Z) = H_\lambda(Z) + \E[\ln \tfrac{\dd\nu}{\dd\lambda}(Z)]$.
Expanding the joint entropy of $(Y, Z)$ against $\rho \otimes \lambda$ as $H_\rho(Y) + H_\lambda(Z \mid Y) = H_\lambda(Z) + H_\rho(Y \mid Z)$ and substituting gives \eqref{eq:classes}.
Since consistency fixes the law of $Z$ given $Y$ to $\iota(Y)$ under every policy, every term is a functional of the law of $Y$.
A closed and sufficient belief reduces the regulation problem over histories to a controlled Markov process on beliefs \citep{Kaelbling-planning98}.
Its occupancies form a convex and compact set \citep[Remark~6]{Feinberg-constrained20}, whose extreme points are those of the deterministic stationary policies \citep[Theorem~1]{Piunovskiy-extreme24}, as \citet[Ch.~7, p.~94]{Derman-finite70ch7} shows for the long-run occupancy of a finite-valued belief.
Since $\pz$ is linear in $\occ$ and $H_\nu$ is concave in $\pz$, the criterion $H_\nu$ attains its infimum at an extreme point \citep{Bauer-minimalstellen58}.
\hfill$\square$

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:alignment}}
\label{sup:proof-alignment}
\emph{Part 1.}
By the chain rule for Radon--Nikodym derivatives, $\tfrac{\dd p}{\dd\nu} = \tfrac{\dd p}{\dd\lambda} \tfrac{\dd\lambda}{\dd\nu}$, so $-\ln \tfrac{\dd p}{\dd\nu} = -\ln \tfrac{\dd p}{\dd\lambda} + \ln \tfrac{\dd\nu}{\dd\lambda}$.
Integrating against $p$ gives $H_\nu(p) = H_\lambda(p) + \E_p\big[\ln \tfrac{\dd\nu}{\dd\lambda}\big]$.

\emph{Part 2.}
Part 1 applied to $\nu_\beta$, with $\ln \tfrac{\dd\nu_\beta}{\dd\lambda} = \beta \ln \tfrac{\dd\nu}{\dd\lambda}$, gives $H_{\nu_\beta}(p) = H_\lambda(p) + \beta\, \E_p\big[\ln \tfrac{\dd\nu}{\dd\lambda}\big]$.

\emph{Part 3.}
For each $\beta$ the criterion $H_{\nu_\beta}$ is concave in the occupancy, because the event law is linear in it and $H_\lambda$ is concave while the second term is linear, so a minimiser exists among the finitely many vertices, the deterministic stationary policies.
Let $V$ be that finite set, $\pi_\nu \in V$ minimise $\E_\pi\big[\ln \tfrac{\dd\nu}{\dd\lambda}(Z)\big]$ with value $g^\star$, and $\delta > 0$ the smallest gap $\E_\pi\big[\ln \tfrac{\dd\nu}{\dd\lambda}(Z)\big] - g^\star$ over vertices $\pi$ with a strictly larger value.
On finite $\Zcal$ with counting $\lambda$, $0 \le H_\lambda \le \ln |\Zcal|$, so for $\beta > \beta_0 := \ln|\Zcal| / \delta$ the second term grows by more than $\beta\delta > \ln|\Zcal|$ on every vertex with a larger value, which puts its $H_{\nu_\beta}$ strictly above that of $\pi_\nu$.
Among the vertices that attain $g^\star$ the second term is the same constant, so they are ordered by $H_\lambda$ alone.

\add{For the good set $r = \mathbf 1_G$, so $\E[r(Z)] = \Pr(Z \in G)$ and $r_\Ycal(y) = \iota(y)(G)$, and the last claim is Part 3 for this $r$.}
\hfill$\square$

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:hidden-volume}}
\label{sup:proof-hidden-volume}
\add{On standard spaces $\mu$ disintegrates over $J$ as $\mu(\dd\omega) = \lambda(\dd z)\, \mu_z(\dd\omega)$, with $\mu_z$ carried by $J^{-1}(z)$ and of total mass $v(z) = \tfrac{\dd\nu}{\dd\lambda}(z)$.}
\add{If the fine state follows $\mu_z / v(z)$ given $Z = z$, its law has density $\tfrac{\dd p}{\dd\lambda}(z) / v(z)$ against $\mu$, with $p$ the law of $Z$, so its entropy against $\mu$ is $H_\lambda(Z) + \E[\ln v(Z)] = H_\nu(Z)$ by Part 1 of the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:alignment}.}
\add{The same computation with $\iota(y)$ in place of $p$ gives the entropy $H_\lambda(\iota(y)) - r_\Ycal(y)$ of the fine state behind a prediction $y$.}
\add{For existence take $\Omega = \{(z,u) : 0 \le u \le e^{-r(z)}\}$ with $\mu = \lambda \otimes \mathrm{Leb}$ and $J(z,u) = z$, which has $v = e^{-r}$, and $n_z$ points behind each outcome under a counting measure have $v(z) = n_z$.}
\hfill$\square$

\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{prop:split}}
\label{sup:proof-split}
The direct proof follows, and the derivation from Proposition~\ref{prop:split-pomdp} is given in the main text.
Write $\ell := \ln \tfrac{\dd\nu}{\dd\lambda}$, so that Proposition~\ref{prop:alignment} reads $H_\nu(q) = H_\lambda(q) + \E_q[\ell]$ for every law $q$, in particular for the law of $Z$ given each class.
The left-hand side is fixed and each line names the step that produces it.
\begin{align*}
 H_\nu(Z) &= H_\lambda(Z) + \E[\ell(Z)] && \text{Proposition~\ref{prop:alignment}} \\
      &= H_\rho(\tilde Z) + H_\lambda(Z \mid \tilde Z) - H_\rho(\tilde Z \mid Z) + \E[\ell(Z)] && \text{chain rule on } (\tilde Z, Z) \text{, both orders} \\
      &= H_\rho(\tilde Z) + H_\nu(Z \mid \tilde Z) - H_\rho(\tilde Z \mid Z) && \text{Proposition~\ref{prop:alignment} given each class} \\
      &= I(S;\tilde Z) + I(A;\tilde Z \mid S) + H_\nu(Z \mid \tilde Z) - H_\rho(\tilde Z \mid Z) && \text{chain rule on } H_\rho(\tilde Z).
\end{align*}
The second line uses $H_\rho(\tilde Z) + H_\lambda(Z \mid \tilde Z) = H_\lambda(Z) + H_\rho(\tilde Z \mid Z)$, both sides being the entropy of the pair against $\rho \otimes \lambda$, whose density factorises in either order.
The third line uses $\E[\ell(Z)] = \E\big[\E[\ell(Z) \mid \tilde Z]\big]$.
The last line is the chain rule $H_\rho(\tilde Z) = I(S;\tilde Z) + I(A;\tilde Z \mid S) + H_\rho(\tilde Z \mid S,A)$ for counting $\rho$, whose residual is zero because $\tilde Z$ is a function of $(S,A)$.
\hfill$\square$

\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:freebits}}
\label{sup:proof-freebits}
Given $S = s$ the class $\kappa(s,A)$ is a function of $A$, and a function of $A$ is independent of $A$ exactly when it is constant on the support of $\pi(\cdot \mid s)$.
The identity is the definition of conditional mutual information, and by Corollary~\ref{prop:split} only $I(A;\tilde Z \mid S)$ enters the criterion.
\hfill$\square$

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:split-pomdp}}
\label{sup:proof-split-pomdp}
The first step of the proof of Corollary~\ref{prop:split} holds unchanged, since it uses only that $Z$ given $\tilde Z$ has law $\tilde Z$.
The chain rule on $H_\rho(\tilde Z)$ over $(X, A, P, \kappa, S)$ in that order gives the five information terms plus the residual $H_\rho(\tilde Z \mid X,A,P,\kappa,S)$.
The residual is zero because $\tilde Z = \kappa(S,A)$ is a function of $(S,A,\theta)$.
Part 1 of Proposition~\ref{prop:alignment} within each class splits $H_\nu(Z \mid \tilde Z)$ into the noise and the goal.
\hfill$\square$

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:budget}}
\label{sup:proof-budget}
\emph{Right inequality.}
Write $p_T := \Pr(B = 1 \mid X_T)$.
Deciding $B$ with error at most $\delta$ means $p_T \le \delta$ or $p_T \ge 1 - \delta$ almost surely, and since $h$ is symmetric about $\tfrac12$ and increasing on $[0, \tfrac12]$ this gives $H(B \mid X_T) = \E[h(p_T)] \le h(\delta)$.
Hence $I(B; X_T) = h(q) - H(B \mid X_T) \ge h(q) - h(\delta)$, and $I(\theta; X_T) \ge I(B; X_T)$ because $B$ is a function of $\theta$.
By the chain rule over the steps, $I(\theta; X_T) = \sum_{t < T} I(\theta; A_t, Z_t \mid X_t) = \sum_{t < T} I(\theta; Z_t \mid X_t, A_t)$, where $I(\theta; A_t \mid X_t) = 0$ because the action is chosen from the history and independent randomness.

\emph{Left inequality.}
Given $(X_t, A_t)$ the outcome $Z_t$ is drawn from the class $\tilde Z_t$, so $\theta$ and $Z_t$ are independent given $(\tilde Z_t, X_t, A_t)$ and the data processing inequality gives $I(\theta; Z_t \mid X_t, A_t) \le I(\theta; \tilde Z_t \mid X_t, A_t)$.
The chain rule on $\theta = (P, \kappa)$ gives $I(\theta; \tilde Z_t \mid X_t, A_t) = \bar I_{P,t} + \bar I_{\kappa,t}$.
\hfill$\square$

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:plugin}}
\label{sup:proof-plugin}
\emph{Descent.}
For any law $p$ and any $\hat q$ with positive density against $\nu$, $\E_p[-\ln \tfrac{\dd\hat q}{\dd\nu}] = H_\nu(p) + \KL(p \,\|\, \hat q)$, so the inner objective of \eqref{eq:game} with $\hat q_k$ in place of $q$ equals $\Lcal(\pi) + \KL(p_\pi \,\|\, \hat q_k)$ for every policy, the floor terms being exact.
Since $\pi_{k+1}$ minimises it and $\pi_k$ is feasible, $\Lcal(\pi_{k+1}) + \KL(p_{\pi_{k+1}} \,\|\, \hat q_k) \le \Lcal(\pi_k) + \KL(p_{\pi_k} \,\|\, \hat q_k)$, which is \eqref{eq:plugin}.

\emph{Finite trials.}
Let $\hat p_n$ be the empirical law of the outcomes of $n$ trials and let $Z$ be drawn from $\hat p_n$ given $\hat p_n$, so that the marginal of $Z$ is $\E[\hat p_n] = p_\pi$.
Then $H_\nu(Z) = H_\nu(p_\pi)$ and $H_\nu(Z \mid \hat p_n) = \E[H_\nu(\hat p_n)]$, and $I(Z; \hat p_n) = H_\nu(Z) - H_\nu(Z \mid \hat p_n)$ gives the identity, where the change of measure cancels because it is linear in the law.
\hfill$\square$

\section{Caveats}
\label{sup:caveats}

\paragraph{The good set as a limit.}
\add{The good set of Proposition~\ref{prop:alignment} can be read as the limit $\beta \to \infty$, or as an iterative volume assignment in which the change of base sharpens step by step.}
\add{The limit itself is singular, since $\dd\nu_\beta / \dd\lambda = e^{-\beta} \to 0$ on $G$ breaks the condition $\psi_\# \occ \ll \nu$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}.}
\add{The limit is therefore taken on the minimisers, which are the same for every finite $\beta > \beta_0$, and not on $H_{\nu_\beta}$.}
\add{A strict indicator goal may be ill defined along a transient trajectory, which is where the absolute continuity condition of the theorem matters.}

\paragraph{Why the criterion needs an occupancy.}
\add{Conant and Ashby state their criterion for a single draw of $Z$ without time, so a sequential version must choose which outcome law the entropy is taken over.}
\add{The first choice is an entropy reward per state and action, $-H(Z \mid s,a)$.}
\add{This only charges the noise at each pair, and misses that the outcome also varies across the pairs the policy visits, which is the term $I(S,A;Z)$ in $H(Z) = H(Z \mid S,A) + I(S,A;Z)$.}
\add{The second choice is the entropy at each time step, $\sum_t \gamma^t H(Z_t)$, or of the whole trajectory, $H(Z_{1:T})$ \citep{JarneOrnia-predictable25}.}
\add{Both fail on cycles, since a deterministic policy that alternates between two outcomes is perfectly predictable at every step, and so scores zero while it visits two outcomes.}
\add{What both miss is the information that time carries about the outcome, as concavity gives $(1-\gamma)\sum_t \gamma^t H(Z_t) \le H(\psi_\# \occ_\gamma)$.}
\add{The occupancy removes time by averaging over it, and so counts every outcome the policy visits regardless of their order.}
\add{The value function yields the same object, since the value of the indicator reward $\mathbf 1\{\psi(s,a) \in B\}$ is, up to the factor $1-\gamma$, the occupancy pushed through $\psi$, which is the successor representation \citep{Dayan-improving93}.}
