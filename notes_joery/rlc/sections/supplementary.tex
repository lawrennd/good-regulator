\section{Extended measure-theoretic primer}
\label{sup:primer}
Measure theory generalises the notion of counting to volume over mathematical spaces \citep{Billingsley-probability12}.
We use it to express the ideas of the good regulator \citep{Conant-good70} in reinforcement learning terms, and to manipulate entropy, divergences and mutual information as functionals of the laws of random variables.
This section is a brief self-contained primer on the key concepts, since we expect an RL audience to be familiar with probability theory but less so with measure theory.


\paragraph{\add{Measures.}}
\add{A $\sigma$-algebra on $\Zcal$ is a collection of measurable subsets that contains $\Zcal$ and is closed under complements and countable unions, here all subsets of a countable $\Zcal$ or the Borel sets of $\mathbb R^n$ generated by the open sets.}
\add{A measure $m$ assigns each measurable $B$ a volume $m(B) \in [0, \infty]$, with $m(\emptyset) = 0$ and additively over countably many disjoint sets, and a law is a measure with $m(\Zcal) = 1$.}
\add{Our two bases, the counting measure on a countable $\Zcal$ and the Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb R^n$, are $\sigma$-finite, so $\Zcal$ is a countable union of sets of finite volume, and turn $\int g\,\dd m$ into $\sum_z g(z)$ and $\int g(z)\,\dd z$.}
\add{A property holds almost everywhere under $m$ if it fails only on a set of $m$-volume zero, and $\nu$ is absolutely continuous with respect to $\lambda$, $\nu \ll \lambda$, if every set of $\lambda$-volume zero has $\nu$-volume zero.}
\add{For a $\sigma$-finite $\lambda$ and a law $\nu \ll \lambda$ the Radon--Nikodym theorem gives a density $\tfrac{\dd\nu}{\dd\lambda} \ge 0$, unique almost everywhere under $\lambda$, with $\int g\,\dd\nu = \int g\,\tfrac{\dd\nu}{\dd\lambda}\,\dd\lambda$.}
\add{Along a chain $p \ll q \ll m$ densities multiply, $\tfrac{\dd p}{\dd m} = \tfrac{\dd p}{\dd q}\,\tfrac{\dd q}{\dd m}$, so $\tfrac{\dd p}{\dd q} = \tfrac{\dd p}{\dd m} / \tfrac{\dd q}{\dd m}$ almost everywhere under $p$.}

\paragraph{\add{Maps and products.}}
\add{A map $\psi\colon \mathcal X \to \Zcal$ is measurable if pre-images $\psi^{-1}(B)$ of measurable sets are measurable, as we assume of all maps and functions, and it pulls $g$ on $\Zcal$ back to $g \circ \psi$ and pushes $m$ on $\mathcal X$ forward to $\psi_\# m(B) = m(\psi^{-1}(B))$, so $\int g\,\dd(\psi_\# m) = \int g \circ \psi\,\dd m$.}
\add{Two $\sigma$-finite measures $m$ on $\mathcal X$ and $m'$ on $\Zcal$ give the product measure $m \otimes m'$ on $\mathcal X \times \Zcal$ with the $\sigma$-algebra generated by rectangles, the unique measure with ${(m \otimes m')(C \times B) = m(C)\, m'(B)}$.}
\add{A law $p$ on $\mathcal X \times \Zcal$ has as marginals its pushforwards $p_{\mathcal X}$ and $p_{\Zcal}$ under the two projections, so $p_{\mathcal X}(C) = p(C \times \Zcal)$, and $p_{\mathcal X} \otimes p_{\Zcal}$ keeps these marginals but forgets how the coordinates vary together.}

\paragraph{\add{Kernels.}}
\add{A kernel $k$ from $\mathcal X$ to $\Zcal$ is a law $k(\cdot \mid x)$ on $\Zcal$ for each $x$, measurable in $x$, and with a law $p_{\mathcal X}$ it builds the joint law $p(C \times B) = \int_C k(B \mid x)\,\dd p_{\mathcal X}(x)$, the measure form of $p(x,z) = p(x)\, k(z \mid x)$.}
\add{On standard Borel spaces, which include countable sets, $\mathbb R^n$ and their finite products, every joint law disintegrates into $p_{\mathcal X}$ and a conditional kernel $p(\cdot \mid x)$, unique for almost every $x$ under $p_{\mathcal X}$, and transition laws, policies and outcome channels are all kernels.}

\paragraph{\add{Entropy.}}
\add{The entropy of a law $p \ll m$ against a base $m$ is $H_m(p) = -\int \ln \tfrac{\dd p}{\dd m}\,\dd p = -\int \tfrac{\dd p}{\dd m} \ln \tfrac{\dd p}{\dd m}\,\dd m$, the Shannon entropy under counting $m$, where the density is the mass $p(\{z\})$, and the differential entropy under Lebesgue $m$.}
\add{It may be infinite, and every identity below is read where the entropies involved are finite.}
\add{Since $\tfrac{\dd p}{\dd p} = 1$ we have $H_p(p) = 0$, and for $p \ll q \ll m$ the quotient of densities gives the change of base $H_q(p) = H_m(p) + \E_p[\ln \tfrac{\dd q}{\dd m}]$.}
\add{For a law $q$ this is the divergence $H_q(p) = -\KL(p \,\|\, q)$, and Gibbs' inequality $\KL(p \,\|\, q) \ge 0$ with equality only at $q = p$ gives $H_m(p) = \min_q \E_p[-\ln \tfrac{\dd q}{\dd m}]$ over laws $q \ll m$, with $-\ln 0 = +\infty$.}
\add{As a minimum of functionals linear in $p$, $H_m$ is concave where finite.}

\paragraph{\add{Conditional entropy.}}
\add{For a joint law $p$ with $p(\cdot \mid x) \ll m'$ for almost every $x$ under $p_{\mathcal X}$, the conditional entropy $H_{m'}(p \mid \mathcal X) = \int H_{m'}(p(\cdot \mid x))\,\dd p_{\mathcal X}(x)$ averages the entropy of the kernel, so only the conditioned coordinate carries a base.}
\add{A point-mass kernel, as for a function $z = \psi(x)$, has zero conditional entropy against the counting measure and none against the Lebesgue measure, to which it has no density.}
\add{For $p \ll m \otimes m'$ the density factors almost everywhere as $\tfrac{\dd p}{\dd (m \otimes m')}(x,z) = \tfrac{\dd p_{\mathcal X}}{\dd m}(x)\, \tfrac{\dd p(\cdot \mid x)}{\dd m'}(z)$, and taking $-\E_p \ln$ gives the chain rule $H_{m \otimes m'}(p) = H_m(p_{\mathcal X}) + H_{m'}(p \mid \mathcal X)$, or $H_{m'}(p_{\Zcal}) + H_m(p \mid \Zcal)$ along the other coordinate.}

\paragraph{\add{Mutual information.}}
\add{Mutual information is the negative entropy against the product of the marginals, $I(p) = -H_{p_{\mathcal X} \otimes p_{\Zcal}}(p) = \KL(p \,\|\, p_{\mathcal X} \otimes p_{\Zcal})$, set to $+\infty$ when $p$ is not absolutely continuous to that product, so it depends on which coordinates are separated but on no base.}
\add{For $p \ll m \otimes m'$ the density of $p_{\mathcal X} \otimes p_{\Zcal}$ against $m \otimes m'$ is the product of the marginal densities, so the change of base gives $I(p) = H_m(p_{\mathcal X}) + H_{m'}(p_{\Zcal}) - H_{m \otimes m'}(p)$, and the chain rule gives $I(p) = H_{m'}(p_{\Zcal}) - H_{m'}(p \mid \mathcal X) = H_m(p_{\mathcal X}) - H_m(p \mid \Zcal)$.}

\paragraph{\add{Random variables.}}
\add{A random variable $Z$ is a measurable map from a probability space $(\Omega, \mathcal F, \Pr)$ into $\Zcal$ with law $Z_\# \Pr$, a pair $(X,Z)$ has a joint law on $\mathcal X \times \Zcal$ whose marginals are the laws of $X$ and $Z$, and $Z = \psi(S,A)$ has the pushforward under $\psi$ of the joint law of $(S,A)$.}
\add{We write $H_m(Z)$, $H_{m'}(Z \mid X)$ and $I(X;Z)$ for the functionals above on the joint law of the named variables, each entropy carrying the base of the variable left of the bar, so the chain rules read $H_{m \otimes m'}(X,Z) = H_m(X) + H_{m'}(Z \mid X)$ and $I(X;Z) = H_{m'}(Z) - H_{m'}(Z \mid X)$.}
\add{The conditional $I(X;Z \mid Y)$ averages the mutual information of the joint law of $(X,Z)$ given $Y = y$ under the law of $Y$, with $I(X;Z,Y) = I(X;Y) + I(X;Z \mid Y)$.}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion}}
\label{sup:proof-criterion}
First apply the change of base for $\nu \ll \lambda$, which gives $H_\nu(Z) = H_\lambda(Z) + \E[\ln \tfrac{\dd\nu}{\dd\lambda}(Z)]$.
Expanding the joint entropy of $(Y, Z)$ against $\rho \otimes \lambda$ as $H_\rho(Y) + H_\lambda(Z \mid Y) = H_\lambda(Z) + H_\rho(Y \mid Z)$ and substituting gives \eqref{eq:classes}.
Since consistency fixes the law of $Z$ given $Y$ to $\iota(Y)$ under every policy, every term is a functional of the law of $Y$.
A closed and sufficient belief reduces the regulation problem over histories to a controlled Markov process on beliefs \citep{Kaelbling-planning98}.
Its occupancies form a convex and compact set \citep[Remark~6]{Feinberg-constrained20}, whose extreme points are those of the deterministic stationary policies \citep[Theorem~1]{Piunovskiy-extreme24}, as \citet[Ch.~7, p.~94]{Derman-finite70ch7} shows for the long-run occupancy of a finite-valued belief.
Since $\pz$ is linear in $\occ$ and $H_\nu$ is concave in $\pz$, the criterion $H_\nu$ attains its infimum at an extreme point \citep{Bauer-minimalstellen58}.
\hfill$\square$

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:alignment}}
\label{sup:proof-alignment}
\emph{Part 1.}
By the chain rule for Radon--Nikodym derivatives, $\tfrac{\dd p}{\dd\nu} = \tfrac{\dd p}{\dd\lambda} \tfrac{\dd\lambda}{\dd\nu}$, so $-\ln \tfrac{\dd p}{\dd\nu} = -\ln \tfrac{\dd p}{\dd\lambda} + \ln \tfrac{\dd\nu}{\dd\lambda}$.
Integrating against $p$ gives $H_\nu(p) = H_\lambda(p) + \E_p\big[\ln \tfrac{\dd\nu}{\dd\lambda}\big]$.

\emph{Part 2.}
Part 1 applied to $\nu_\beta$, with $\ln \tfrac{\dd\nu_\beta}{\dd\lambda} = \beta \ln \tfrac{\dd\nu}{\dd\lambda}$, gives $H_{\nu_\beta}(p) = H_\lambda(p) + \beta\, \E_p\big[\ln \tfrac{\dd\nu}{\dd\lambda}\big]$.

\emph{Part 3.}
For each $\beta$ the criterion $H_{\nu_\beta}$ is concave in the occupancy, because the event law is linear in it and $H_\lambda$ is concave while the second term is linear, so a minimiser exists among the finitely many vertices, the deterministic stationary policies.
Let $V$ be that finite set, $\pi_\nu \in V$ minimise $\E_\pi\big[\ln \tfrac{\dd\nu}{\dd\lambda}(Z)\big]$ with value $g^\star$, and $\delta > 0$ the smallest gap $\E_\pi\big[\ln \tfrac{\dd\nu}{\dd\lambda}(Z)\big] - g^\star$ over vertices $\pi$ with a strictly larger value.
On finite $\Zcal$ with counting $\lambda$, $0 \le H_\lambda \le \ln |\Zcal|$, so for $\beta > \beta_0 := \ln|\Zcal| / \delta$ the second term grows by more than $\beta\delta > \ln|\Zcal|$ on every vertex with a larger value, which puts its $H_{\nu_\beta}$ strictly above that of $\pi_\nu$.
Among the vertices that attain $g^\star$ the second term is the same constant, so they are ordered by $H_\lambda$ alone.

\add{For the good set $r = \mathbf 1_G$, so $\E[r(Z)] = \Pr(Z \in G)$ and $r_\Ycal(y) = \iota(y)(G)$, and the last claim is Part 3 for this $r$.}
\hfill$\square$

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:hidden-volume}}
\label{sup:proof-hidden-volume}
\add{On standard spaces $\mu$ disintegrates over $J$ as $\mu(\dd\omega) = \lambda(\dd z)\, \mu_z(\dd\omega)$, with $\mu_z$ carried by $J^{-1}(z)$ and of total mass $v(z) = \tfrac{\dd\nu}{\dd\lambda}(z)$.}
\add{If the fine state follows $\mu_z / v(z)$ given $Z = z$, its law has density $\tfrac{\dd p}{\dd\lambda}(z) / v(z)$ against $\mu$, with $p$ the law of $Z$, so its entropy against $\mu$ is $H_\lambda(Z) + \E[\ln v(Z)] = H_\nu(Z)$ by Part 1 of the proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:alignment}.}
\add{The same computation with $\iota(y)$ in place of $p$ gives the entropy $H_\lambda(\iota(y)) - r_\Ycal(y)$ of the fine state behind a prediction $y$.}
\add{For existence take $\Omega = \{(z,u) : 0 \le u \le e^{-r(z)}\}$ with $\mu = \lambda \otimes \mathrm{Leb}$ and $J(z,u) = z$, which has $v = e^{-r}$, and $n_z$ points behind each outcome under a counting measure have $v(z) = n_z$.}
\hfill$\square$

\paragraph{\add{The combined term as a belief shift.}}
\add{Write $W = (\hat\theta, \hat S)$, with all laws those of the occupancy under the regulator's model as in the proof above.}
\add{The chain rule gives $I(W;Z \mid X,A) = I(\hat\theta;Z \mid X,A) + I(\hat S;Z \mid X,A,\hat\theta) = I_{\hat\theta} + I_{\hat S}$.}
\add{By definition and Bayes' rule, $p(W,Z \mid X,A) = p(W \mid X,A,Z)\,p(Z \mid X,A)$, so}
\begin{equation*}
\color{red}
  I(W;Z \mid X,A) = \E\Big[\ln \frac{p(W,Z \mid X,A)}{p(W \mid X,A)\,p(Z \mid X,A)}\Big] = \E\Big[\ln \frac{p(W \mid X,A,Z)}{p(W \mid X,A)}\Big].
\end{equation*}
\add{Taking the expectation over $W$ given $(X,A,Z)$ first, the inner expectation is $\KL\big(p(W \mid X,A,Z) \,\|\, p(W \mid X,A)\big)$, and the outer expectation over $(X,A,Z)$ gives the identity.}
\add{The ratios are densities of the posterior against the prior, which exist almost surely when the mutual information is finite, and both sides are $+\infty$ otherwise.}
\add{The same three steps with $W = A$, the outcome $Y$ and the conditioning $X$ give $I_A = \E\big[\KL\big(p(A \mid X,Y) \,\|\, \pi(A \mid X)\big)\big] = H(A \mid X) - H(A \mid X,Y)$, so both empowerment terms are the expected shift from a prior to a posterior over an affordance.}
\hfill$\square$

\subsection{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:budget}}
\label{sup:proof-budget}
\add{All laws are those of the regulator's model, in which the hypothesis and the initial latent state $(\hat\theta, \hat S_0)$ follow the initial belief, the latent state moves by the transition of the hypothesis, each outcome $Z_t$ follows the likelihood given $(\hat S_t, \hat\theta, A_t)$, the history holds only actions and outcomes, and the initial history carries no information.}
\add{The policy chooses the action from the history and independent randomness, which covers every policy that acts on the belief, since the belief is a function of the history.}
\add{The target $W_t = f(\hat\theta, \hat S_t)$ takes finitely many values, entropies are in nats, and where the target refers to $\hat\Theta_c$ that set is fixed in advance for a candidate $(\pi^\star, q^\star)$.}
\add{The rate $\bar\Delta$ of the proposition is the average over the run of $\Delta_t = H(W_{t+1} \mid X_{t+1}) - H(W_t \mid X_{t+1})$, the doubt that a transition of the latent state adds.}
\add{Write $h_t = H(W_t \mid X_t = x)$ for the entropy of the target under the belief at a history, so that the doubt is $D_t = \E[h_t]$ with the expectation over the histories.}

\add{\emph{One step.} Write $i_W(x,a) = I(W_t; Z_t \mid X_t = x, A_t = a)$ for the information at a given history and action.}
\add{The action carries no information about the target given the history, so the definition of mutual information gives $\E\big[H(W_t \mid X_{t+1} = \cdot\,) \mid X_t, A_t\big] = h_t - i_W(X_t, A_t)$.}
\add{With $\delta_t = H(W_{t+1} \mid X_{t+1} = \cdot\,) - H(W_t \mid X_{t+1} = \cdot\,)$ the entropy that the transition adds at the new history, $\E[h_{t+1} \mid X_t, A_t] = h_t - i_W(X_t, A_t) + \E[\delta_t \mid X_t, A_t]$.}

\add{\emph{The run.} By the previous step, $M_t = h_t + \sum_{s<t} \big(i_W(X_s, A_s) - \delta_s\big)$ is a martingale with respect to the history and the independent randomness, with bounded increments because the target is finite-valued.}
\add{The time $\tau$ is a stopping time of finite mean, so optional stopping gives $\E[M_\tau] = M_0$, that is $\E\big[\sum_{t<\tau} i_W(X_t, A_t)\big] = D_0 - D_\tau + \E\big[\sum_{t<\tau} \delta_t\big]$ with $D_\tau = \E[h_\tau]$.}
\add{An end of the run that depends on the hidden state must be visible in the outcome, so that it is decided by the history, and what it reveals is then counted in $i_W$.}
\add{The occupancy of the run is the law of the visited $(X_t, A_t, Z_t, \hat\theta, \hat S_t)$ for $t < \tau$, normalised by $\E[\tau]$.}
\add{The history determines the step, so a conditional mutual information given $(X,A)$ under this occupancy is the average of the per-history terms, $I(W; Z \mid X, A) = \E\big[\sum_{t<\tau} i_W(X_t, A_t)\big] / \E[\tau]$, and $\bar\Delta = \E\big[\sum_{t<\tau} \delta_t\big] / \E[\tau]$.}
\add{This gives the equality of \eqref{eq:budget}.}

\add{\emph{The learner terms.} The target is a function of $(\hat\theta, \hat S_t)$, so data processing gives $i_W \le I(\hat\theta, \hat S_t; Z_t \mid X_t = x, A_t = a)$, with equality when the target is the pair.}
\add{The chain rule splits the right side into the per-history terms of $I_{\hat\theta}$ and $I_{\hat S}$, which gives the inequality of \eqref{eq:budget}, and by the paragraph above it is the expected shift of the belief.}
\add{For a target of the hypothesis alone, $W_{t+1} = W_t$ and $\delta_t = 0$, and data processing gives $i_W \le I(\hat\theta; Z_t \mid X_t = x, A_t = a)$, so $\E[\tau]\, I_{\hat\theta} \ge D_0 - D_\tau$.}

\add{\emph{The floor.} Rearranging, $D_\tau \ge D_0 + \E[\tau]\,\bar\Delta - \E[\tau]\,(I_{\hat\theta} + I_{\hat S})$.}
\add{If $I_{\hat\theta} + I_{\hat S} \le c_{\hat\theta} + c_{\hat S}$ and $D_\tau \le d$, then $c_{\hat\theta} + c_{\hat S} \ge (D_0 - d)/\E[\tau] + \bar\Delta$, and for a target of the hypothesis alone $c_{\hat\theta} \ge (D_0 - d)/\E[\tau]$.}
\add{The rate $\bar\Delta$ belongs to the policy that is run and can be negative, for instance when the transitions send the latent state to a known value.}

\add{\emph{Discount.} Let the run end with probability $1-\gamma$ after each step, independently of everything else.}
\add{Then $\Pr(\tau > t) = \gamma^t$ and $\E[\tau] = 1/(1-\gamma)$, the occupancy of the run is the discounted occupancy over histories, and $D_\tau = (1-\gamma) \sum_t \gamma^t D_{t+1}$ is the discounted average of the doubt after each step.}
\add{If runs repeat, each from the initial belief and an empty history, the long-run occupancy is the occupancy of one run by the renewal theorem.}

\add{\emph{Ceilings.} For a countable hypothesis class the total information about the hypothesis is at most the entropy of the initial belief, $\E[\tau]\, I_{\hat\theta} \le H(\hat\theta)$, so no floor on $I_{\hat\theta}$ above $H(\hat\theta)/\E[\tau]$ can be met.}
\add{Each term is also at most the largest per-history information, which is what one outcome can carry.}
\add{For a run that never ends, the average of the per-history terms of $I_{\hat\theta}$ over the first $T$ steps is at most $H(\hat\theta)/T$ and goes to zero.}

\add{\emph{Disturbance.} For a run of $T$ steps the equality reads $\tfrac1T \sum_{t<T} I(W_t; Z_t \mid X_t, A_t) = \bar\Delta + (D_0 - D_T)/T$, and the last term vanishes as $T$ grows because the doubt is bounded.}
\add{The same holds when the hypothesis $\hat\theta_t$ itself changes between steps, with $\Delta_t$ then counting that change as well.}
\add{One case is a hypothesis that is redrawn from the initial belief with probability $\varepsilon$ after each step, independently of the history and unseen by the regulator, with membership of a set as the target.}
\add{Given $X_{t+1}$ the mass on the set moves from $p_t^+$ to $(1-\varepsilon)\,p_t^+ + \varepsilon\,p_0$, and the binary entropy $h$ is concave, so $\Delta_t \ge \varepsilon\,(D_0 - D_t^+)$ with $D_t^+ = H(W_t \mid X_{t+1})$ the doubt after the outcome.}
\add{The long-run average then satisfies $I_{\hat\theta} \ge \varepsilon\,(D_0 - \bar D)$ with $\bar D$ the long-run average of $D_t^+$, which is the floor of the proposition with $1/\varepsilon$ for the expected length.}

\add{\emph{The action.} Under the occupancy of the run, the chain rule on $I(\hat\theta, A; Z \mid X)$ in both orders gives $I(A; Z \mid X) + I(\hat\theta; Z \mid X, A) = I(\hat\theta; Z \mid X) + I(A; Z \mid X, \hat\theta)$.}
\add{For a deterministic policy the action is a function of the history, so $I_A = 0$ and both action terms vanish, while $I_{\hat\theta} = I(\hat\theta; Z \mid X)$ can be positive.}

\add{\emph{Entropy search.} With the target the optimum under the hypothesis, $i_W(x,a)$ is the expected information that the outcome of action $a$ gives about the optimum at the history $x$, which is the quantity that entropy search maximises over $a$, and the middle term of \eqref{eq:budget} is its sum over the run.}
\hfill$\square$

\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:residual}}
\label{sup:proof-residual}
\add{Write $E = \mathbf 1\{\hat\theta \in \hat\Theta(\pi^\star)\}$, with all laws those of the regulator's model as in the proof above.}
\add{Conditioning cannot raise entropy, so $D_t = H(B \mid X_t) \ge H(B \mid X_t, E)$.}
\add{Outside the consistent set $B = 0$, because $\hat\Theta_c \subseteq \hat\Theta(\pi^\star)$, so $H(B \mid X_t, E) = \Pr(E = 1)\, H(B \mid X_t, E = 1)$.}
\add{The probability of a history under a hypothesis is the product over its steps of the probability of each action given the history, which does not depend on the hypothesis, and of each outcome given the history and the action.}
\add{The policy takes only actions that $\pi^\star$ takes, and the hypotheses of $\hat\Theta(\pi^\star)$ agree on the outcome law of those actions at every history, so every history has the same probability under each of them.}
\add{Hence given $E = 1$ the history is independent of the hypothesis, and $H(B \mid X_t, E = 1) = H(B \mid E = 1)$.}
\add{Together, $D_t \ge \Pr(E = 1)\, H(B \mid E = 1) = H(B \mid E)$ under the initial belief, which we call the residual doubt.}
\add{Agreement along $\pi^\star$ is stronger than Definition~\ref{def:confusing}, which asks only that the outcome law of the occupancy matches, and it is what a regulator that learns from histories needs.}
\add{The bound does not say that a deviation removes the doubt, only that nothing else can.}
\hfill$\square$

\paragraph{Why the criterion needs an occupancy.}
\add{Conant and Ashby state their criterion for a single draw of $Z$ without time, so a sequential version must choose which outcome law the entropy is taken over.}
\add{The first choice is an entropy reward per state and action, $-H(Z \mid s,a)$.}
\add{This only charges the noise at each pair, and misses that the outcome also varies across the pairs the policy visits, which is the term $I(S,A;Z)$ in $H(Z) = H(Z \mid S,A) + I(S,A;Z)$.}
\add{The second choice is the entropy at each time step, $\sum_t \gamma^t H(Z_t)$, or of the whole trajectory, $H(Z_{1:T})$ \citep{JarneOrnia-predictable25}.}
\add{Both fail on cycles, since a deterministic policy that alternates between two outcomes is perfectly predictable at every step, and so scores zero while it visits two outcomes.}
\add{What both miss is the information that time carries about the outcome, as concavity gives $(1-\gamma)\sum_t \gamma^t H(Z_t) \le H(\psi_\# \occ_\gamma)$.}
\add{The occupancy removes time by averaging over it, and so counts every outcome the policy visits regardless of their order.}
\add{The value function yields the same object, since the value of the indicator reward $\mathbf 1\{\psi(s,a) \in B\}$ is, up to the factor $1-\gamma$, the occupancy pushed through $\psi$, which is the successor representation \citep{Dayan-improving93}.}
