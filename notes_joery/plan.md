Mirrored in Notion: https://app.notion.com/p/Homeostasis-as-an-RL-criterion-project-plan-3e3ce0ba7905809c99dde0d6eeeb53b9 (this file is the source; copy changes there).

## 0. The idea in five lines

An agent's long-run state–action frequencies (the occupancy `d^π`) are pushed through a fixed read-out channel `K(z | s, a)` to give the long-run distribution `p_π` of an outcome `z`. Following Conant and Ashby's good-regulator theorem, the agent is asked to make that outcome distribution as concentrated as possible: minimise the entropy of `p_π`. This is a concave objective over the occupancy polytope, so a deterministic policy is optimal, but it is not the return of any fixed reward, so it is not an ordinary MDP. Depending on the channel it either reproduces standard reward maximisation, or expresses regulation against a disturbance with no reward at all. The project asks what this buys, where it fails, and how far it reaches.

## 1. Where we are (22 September 2026)

Theory, written up in the Overleaf notes:

- Objective, occupancy polytope (discounted and average-reward), deterministic optimum, concavity, local optima.
- Reward recovered two ways. Through a stochastic channel that emits a "good" outcome with probability equal to the reward and a uniform noise outcome otherwise: the entropy objective then depends on the policy only through the return, and its minimiser is the return maximiser exactly when the noise alphabet is large enough relative to the best return (binary entropy of the best return below best return times log alphabet size). Through the reference measure: a non-uniform reference adds an ordinary expected cost to the entropy.
- General outcome spaces: entropy relative to a reference measure, desirable set of positive volume, the alphabet-size threshold becomes a volume ratio, a finiteness floor (the channel may not concentrate on zero volume), and a continuous set-point task where the entropy minimiser holds the centre of the desirable band.
- Algorithm: majorisation–minimisation. Freeze a model `q̂` of the outcome distribution, solve the MDP with reward `ln q̂(z)` by one natural-gradient actor step on successor features, refit `q̂`, repeat. Monotone descent up to model error. On the stochastic reward channel this collapses to SARSA on the original reward with a scalar time-varying gain that is positive only once the estimated return exceeds the volume fraction of the desirable set.
- Entropy budget: `H[Z] = I(S;Z) + I(A;Z|S) + H[Z|S,A]`. The middle term is one-step empowerment, and the objective minimises it. For the reward channel with binary reward the first two terms together are at most `ln 2`, so empowerment carries no signal before the reward does.

Experiments, all on DeepSea (tabular, pure JAX, code in `deepsea_homeostatis`):

- The alphabet-size threshold is real: above it the entropy minimiser seeks the treasure, below it avoids it.
- Empowerment as a bonus with weight `κ`: for `κ ≥ 0` the treasure-cell policy stops at a Boltzmann temperature `σ(ΔQ/κ)`, predicted to three decimals; first discovery is unchanged; for `κ < 0` the agent is pushed away from the treasure and never returns. Empowerment is a targeted temperature, not exploration.
- Regulation is 3% of the objective on DeepSea. It is a good exploration testbed and a poor regulation testbed.

Not done: any epistemic exploration mechanism; any continuous task; any function approximation; anything on representations; any multi-agent setting; related work beyond a handful of citations.

## 2. The four parts

### Part 1. Background and positioning (accumulating, TBD)

Goal: know at all times what we are and are not claiming relative to cybernetics, surprise-minimising RL, convex/unsupervised RL, empowerment, and control as inference.

Rule: every paper we read gets one line in the log in Section 6 the day we read it, with what it does and how we differ. No prose until the writing phase.

### Part 2. Base theory and its RL reading (current, to be closed first)

Goal: from the good-regulator criterion to a working tabular algorithm, with the conditions under which it recovers RL, the conditions under which it expresses regulation, and one example of each.

Done: everything in Section 1 above.

Open branches:

- (2a) Empowerment, theoretical. Finished as a direction: it is a summand of the objective, minimised not maximised, sparse as the reward, and as a bonus only a temperature. Remaining: state the contrast with Klyubin's capacity-based empowerment in one paragraph, and the order-dependence of the budget's two information terms.
- (2b) Forcing the framework to do RL: epistemic exploration. The objective has no exploration in it. Proposed mechanism: a posterior over the channel (Beta on each pair's probability of a good outcome). Wentworth's "gooder regulator" theorem gives the anchor: when the channel is unknown, the minimal thing a regulator must carry is its posterior over the channel, so acting on that posterior is what the good-regulator criterion itself asks for, not an add-on. Minimising the *expected* entropy under the posterior instead of the entropy of the *predictive* distribution differs by an information gain term (predictive entropy = expected entropy + information one outcome carries about the channel). On the reward channel that term is the posterior variance of the return under the policy. Correction, 22 September: it *is* capped by `ln 2`, like the empowerment term; the difference is that it is non-zero wherever the posterior is wide, before any reward has been seen, whereas the empowerment term is zero until one has. The predictive objective also explores on its own through the optimistic prior mean, so it is a mandatory baseline. First implementation: Thompson sampling, draw a channel from the posterior, solve the entropy minimisation exactly for that draw (posterior sampling on the sampled reward, with the sign set by the threshold), act, update. Derivations in the Notion journal (Good regulator project > Journal notes, entry 22 September 2026: https://app.notion.com/p/3e3ce0ba790581ed9369ffcd6c8344fe). Added 23 September: this posterior is over the channel, the model; it is not the belief over the state in (2c), and the text keeps the two apart. Revised 24 September: the objective is the predictive entropy only, taken on the MDP whose state is the posterior (the belief MDP), `min_π H[d^π K̄]` with `K̄` the predictive channel. The occupancy over beliefs carries the learning, so no information term is added; the predictive outcome law equals the true outcome law of the run, so this is the Conant–Ashby criterion with the channel parameter included, not an approximation. On the reward channel it factors through the Bayesian return, so its optimum is the Bayes-optimal policy. The expected entropy is kept only as the static, one-step view. Fixed parameters make the average-reward version degenerate; a parameter redrawn at rate η (the discount read as termination, with the reset hidden) gives a persistent disturbance, and a restless two-arm check shows the optimum returning to the uncertain arm sooner as η grows. Baselines become ε-greedy and Thompson; the predictive-mean planner is now the method. Next object: the unknown general kernel (a categorical outcome law per pair, Dirichlet posterior), with the reward channel as its RL-recovery slice. Derivations in `notes_joery/entropy_budget_empowerment.tex`, Sections 4 and 5.
- (2c) Continuous regulation. The one-dimensional set-point task from the notes, then cart-pole balance from near-upright with the pole angle as outcome and Lebesgue reference on the angle range. Prediction: the policy holds the band centre with a margin that grows with the noise width, with no reward defined. If the angle is observed with noise the policy must act on a belief over the angle, not the raw reading: with memoryless observation-based policies the achievable occupancies are no longer a polytope and the deterministic-optimum result is lost (the entropy analogue of the stochastic-memoryless-POMDP fact). Added 23 September, a later concern for the practical algorithm: the objective is the entropy of the occupancy, an average over infinitely many trials, while a homeostat lives one lifetime, and the two can differ badly (Mutti et al. 2022). When it matters, upper-bound the true occupancy entropy with a running density estimate, as SMiRL does. Corrected 25 September: SMiRL's density is refit within each episode and bounds the trajectory entropy, not the occupancy entropy (Section 6). What upper-bounds the occupancy entropy is the cross-entropy against a density that is fixed while it is scored, as the MM step's frozen q is.
- (2d) Average-reward occupancy side by side with discounted. The average case removes the horizon term from the critic and makes the outcome estimator a plain mean; verify the two agree as the discount tends to one.

Next step: (2b) and (2c) in parallel, both on tabular or near-tabular problems, both with predictions written before the run.

Kill criteria: if (2b) gives no exploration advantage over `ε`-greedy *and* over the predictive-mean planner on DeepSea at `L = 20`, the "epistemic" story is dropped and exploration is stated as out of scope; a pessimistic prior (Beta(1,3)) is run to separate the variance bonus from the optimism of the mean. If (2c) needs any reward shaping to balance, the regulation story is restricted to the discrete case.

### Part 3. Representation learning and inverse-regularised dynamics (exploratory, open)

Goal: understand what the homeostasis objective means when the outcome `z` is a learned representation rather than a fixed sensor. The companion draft proposes action-sufficient representations trained with forward and inverse dynamics losses, keeping only what matters for control. Imposing entropy minimisation on such a space has not been thought through.

Open questions, in the order to take them:

- What is the reference measure on a learned latent space, and who chooses it. Without one the objective is not defined; with a learned one it can be gamed.
- Does the entropy objective collapse the representation (everything maps to one point) and does the inverse-dynamics loss prevent that.
- Whether the channel is the encoder (deterministic, so needs atoms or noise) or a stochastic encoder.
- Which deep-RL setting is the smallest honest test: probably a pixel version of the set-point or cart-pole task from Part 2, so the answer from the sensor case is known.

Next step: one page of theory on the first two questions before any code.

Kill criterion: if by the December gate there is no objective on the latent space that is both well-defined and not trivially collapsing, Part 3 becomes a discussion section.

Added 23 September: before that kill criterion fires, the one-page theory states the fallback. Keep the outcome on the sensor, so the reference measure is inherited and nothing collapses, and ask instead what the latent must keep to act and to learn. At the MM fixed point one reward remains and acting needs only what the optimal action depends on; while the reward is still moving, preserving the successor features of the outcome is sufficient for exact MM steps. This is also why the MM critic uses successor features of the outcome.

### Part 4. Where homeostasis is the point (exploratory, open)

Goal: settings in which the objective is used as is, not bent towards reward.

Candidates:

- The Conant–Ashby situation itself: a persistent disturbance, a regulator, an outcome to hold. The continuous set-point task is the minimal version; a richer one has a disturbance with structure the regulator must model.
- Multi-agent: several regulators sharing an environment, each minimising the entropy of its own outcome. Conjecture to test, not to assume: competing regulators partition the state space into niches. Possible link to open-ended RL.

Next step: after the December gate, one tabular two-agent experiment with a shared disturbance, and nothing else.

Kill criterion: if the two-agent case shows nothing beyond two copies of the single-agent result, Part 4 is reduced to the single-regulator case.

### Part 5. Writing

One paper for RLC 2027. Part 1 positions, Part 2 is the skeleton, Parts 3 and 4 are one concrete instantiation each that shows utility. The risk is too much, not too little: anything beyond one clean instance per part is cut to an appendix or a later paper, and open questions are listed rather than pursued.

## 4. Paper skeleton (working)

1. Regulation as an objective: good regulator, occupancy, channel, entropy.
2. When it is RL: the stochastic reward channel, the threshold, the collapse of the algorithm to reward-scaled SARSA.
3. When it is regulation: continuous outcomes, reference measure, set-point result, cart-pole balance.
4. What it is not: empowerment as a summand, the `ln 2` budget, the temperature result.
5. Algorithm and exploration: majorisation–minimisation, channel posterior.
6. One step each into representations and multiple regulators.
7. Positioning and open questions.

## 5. Working rules

- Predictions before runs, in writing, with the numbers. Runs that contradict them are reported as such.
- Every new symbol in the notes gets one sentence on why an existing one does not do.
- Derivations are shown, not asserted.
- Experiments run in the background; results are discussed, not narrated.
- Nothing is committed to the code repository without an explicit request.
- Scope: a branch enters the paper only if it is needed for one instance of utility in its part. Everything else is logged here and left.

## 6. Related-work log (one line per entry, append as read)

- Conant & Ashby 1970, "Every good regulator of a system must be a model of that system". The criterion we start from: minimise the entropy of the regulated outcome; optimal regulators are deterministic functions of the state. We keep the criterion and move it from one decision to an occupancy.
- Klyubin, Polani & Nehaniv 2005, empowerment. Channel capacity from actions to future states, maximised as an intrinsic drive. Ours is one-step mutual information at the policy's own action distribution, a summand of the objective, and minimised.
- Berseth et al. 2021, SMiRL. Surprise minimisation over states: our objective with the identity channel and a learned density; they observe the "do nothing" degeneracy we also discuss. Added 25 September: the density is refit within each episode, so the reward bounds the trajectory entropy H(s_0..s_T) through the chain rule, not the per-step marginals their Eq. 1 states, and not the occupancy entropy. With E the episode index, H(d) = E_E H(d_E) + I(E;S), and SMiRL drops I(E;S): it does not pay for variation across episodes, which our objective does. It also puts the density statistics (θ_t, t) into the state, so the policy plans its effect on its own density, where our MM loop freezes q. Its "unstable environments" are our persistent disturbance, necessarily in P since the channel is the identity. Consequence for v2: "This is the objective of SMiRL" holds for the reward form, not for the distribution the entropy is taken over.
- Papers citing SMiRL, surveyed 25 September (64 found through OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar, an undercount; full triage in the Notion journal entry of that date). The A-tier, each checked against its arXiv abstract:
  - Rhinehart et al. 2021, "Information is power" (IC2), NeurIPS, arXiv 2112.03899. Minimises the entropy of state visitation under a latent variational Bayes filter and argues this makes the agent both gather information and gain control. Nearest published relative with a learned outcome; the baseline for Part 3, and the claim "minimisation induces information gathering" is to be set against our belief-MDP account.
  - Jarne Ornia, Delimpaltadakis, Kober & Alonso-Mora 2025, PARL, TMLR, arXiv 2311.18703. Minimises the trajectory entropy rate h̄ = lim (1/T) h(X_0:T), cast as an average reward, traded off against return. They read SMiRL as stationary-distribution entropy and give the counterexample A↔B, near-zero entropy rate with high stationary entropy (checked in their text). For a stationary process h̄ ≤ H(d), so PARL is the predictability criterion and ours the regulation one: the alternating cycle is free for PARL and costs ln 2 for us. The paper to differentiate from on the occupancy-versus-trajectory axis.
  - Fickinger et al. 2021, "Explore and control with adversarial surprise", arXiv 2107.07394. Two policies on one body play a game over observation surprise, Explore maximising and Control minimising; state coverage proved in block MDPs with stochastic observations. A game-based dark-room fix; candidate baseline for (2b) and a reading for Part 4.
  - Hugessen, Creus Castanyer, Mohamed & Berseth 2024, "Surprise-adaptive intrinsic motivation", RLC, arXiv 2405.17243; Zhao et al. 2022, MOSS, NeurIPS, arXiv 2210.06702. A bandit switching between entropy minimisation and maximisation, and a mixture of a minimising and a maximising policy on URLB. The min/max switching family: empirical answers to the dark room, where ours is structural (persistent disturbance, reference measure, belief state). Baselines for DeepSea and the set-point task; RLC precedent.
  - Hafner, Ortega, Ba, Parr, Friston & Heess 2020, "Action and perception as divergence minimization", arXiv 2009.01791. One divergence objective recovering representation learning, information gain, empowerment and skills, with general agents maximising information with the environment. The taxonomy to place ours in, on the opposite sign for I(Z;E) and empowerment.
  - Furutanpey & Dustdar 2026, "Direction-free intrinsic motivation through epistemic free-energy estimators", arXiv 2607.16858. Parameter information gain plus an aleatoric penalty from expected free energy, surprise minimisation emerging where dynamics are resolved; freezes reward components in windows for a stationary operator, our MM freeze. Rewards information gain where we do not.
  - Lidayan, Dennis & Russell 2025, "BAMDP shaping", ICLR, arXiv 2409.05358. Pseudo-rewards as reward shaping in the Bayes-adaptive MDP, with conditions under which they cannot be hacked. The formal home of our belief-MDP exploration (the BAMDP item in `entropy_budget_empowerment.tex` Section 5).
  - Catenacci Volpi, Greaves, Trendafilov, Salge, Pezzulo & Polani 2023, PLoS Comput. Biol. 19:e1010810, arXiv 2112.11637. Human inverted-pendulum balancing: skill goes with low state entropy and high action entropy (perceptual control theory). Empirical support for (2c). High marginal H(A) is compatible with a deterministic optimal policy, H(A|S) = 0; say so when citing.
  - Ovalle & Lucas 2020, "Modulation of viability signals for self-regulatory control", IWAI, arXiv 2007.09297. Active inference with a self-supervised learned preference distribution, separating environment-induced observations from those that support persistence. Their viability observations are our choice of channel K; the learned preference parallels refitting q.
  - Kaiser & Hamann 2022, "Innate motivation for robot swarms by minimizing surprise", IEEE T-RO 38:3582–3601, arXiv 2405.02579. Each robot evolves an action and predictor pair to minimise its sensor-prediction error; diverse collective behaviours emerge, in simulation and on hardware. Precedent for Part 4, by neuroevolution, not RL.
  - Demoted after checking: EMIX (Suri et al. 2020, arXiv 2009.09842), whose surprise is about value estimates, not an outcome law; Kubota & Kobayashi 2025 (ICDL, SMiRL with adaptive task-reward weighting against local optima), found only as metadata, not read.
- Adjacent to SMiRL, read in full 25 September (notes in the Notion journal entry of that date):
  - Heins, Millidge, Da Costa, Mann, Friston & Couzin 2024, "Collective behavior from surprise minimization", PNAS 121:e2320239121, arXiv 2307.14804. Each agent descends the instantaneous variational free energy of a linear-Gaussian model whose drift has a fixed point η, the set-point on distances to neighbours; action is a deterministic gradient reflex. No expected free energy (absent from the text), no long-run or occupancy argument. Cohesion, alignment and milling emerge; no niche partitioning; perturbations are amplified, not rejected; Conant & Ashby cited without argument. In our terms: the MM inner step with q frozen as a Gaussian at η, never refit. The Part 4 contrast: a shared fixed set-point with symmetric coupling gives consensus, so niches need a refit q, different channels K, or competition for low-entropy outcomes (our reading). Reusable diagnostics: polarisation, angular momentum, fragmentation.
  - Sennesh, Theriault, van de Meent, Barrett & Quigley 2023, "Deriving time-averaged active inference from control principles", IWAI 2022, CCIS pp. 355–370, arXiv 2208.10601. Average-cost KL control with cost −ln R for a reference model R, differential Bellman equation, stochastic Gibbs optimum; no algorithm or experiments. Their Eq. 8 makes the "ergodic" entropy a path entropy rate, not a marginal. With R frozen their objective is our MM inner step; they note in one sentence (Sec. 4) that replacing R by the model gives the long-run average surprise, and do not pursue it. Cite for the average-surprise imperative versus discounted practice and for allostasis vocabulary; their signs are inconsistent (Eqs. 18 vs 26, 22–23) and Eq. 11 is unproved, so reuse nothing without rederiving.
  - Mutti, De Santi & Restelli 2022, "The importance of non-Markovianity in maximum state entropy exploration", ICML, arXiv 2202.03060. Infinite-trial E_∞ = H(d^π) versus single-trial E = E_h H(d_h), E ≤ E_∞; Markovian policies realise every marginal (Thm 3.1), so they suffice for E_∞ (Cor 3.2); deterministic history-dependent policies are optimal single-trial (Thm 4.2); single-trial maximisation over non-Markovian policies is NP-hard (Thm 5.4). For us: Thm 3.1 is objective-free, and our vertex argument sharpens Cor 3.2 to a deterministic Markovian optimum; E_∞ = E + I(h;s_τ) is the SMiRL decomposition, and SMiRL's (θ_t, t) augmentation is the count-based history policy of Thm 4.2. Reasoning from the reading, not yet checked by us: minimisation needs memory only under stochasticity or aliasing, where it commits to the outcome already most frequent; the hardness proof does not transfer; the paper's Lemmas 4.5–4.6 look fragile (the authors' acknowledgements mention a corrected technical error).
  - Yoshida, Daikoku, Nagai & Kuniyoshi 2024, "Emergence of integrated behaviors through direct optimization for homeostasis", Neural Networks 177:106379; Yoshida, Kanazawa & Kuniyoshi 2024, "Synthesising integrated robot behaviour through RL for homeostasis", bioRxiv 10.1101/2024.06.03.597087. PPO on MuJoCo ants and a real quadruped with interoceptive variables (nutrients, energy, motor temperature) under persistent drift; reward is Keramati–Gutkin drive reduction r_t ∝ D(x_t) − D(x_{t+1}), D a squared distance to a set-point, and it beats survival-only and level rewards. Main paper read through abstract, highlights and released code only (full text blocked); companion read in full. The drive-difference return is Φ_0 − ((1−γ)/γ) Σ_{t≥1} γ^t Φ_t with Φ the drive (checked), so it is the discounted set-point cost: our tilted-reference term with zero entropy weight, linear in the occupancy, solved by plain RL. The baseline to beat or explain. For Gaussian outcomes, quadratic cost is ‖bias‖² + tr Cov while entropy is ½ ln det Cov, so the two trade variables off differently. Candidate testbed: a JAX energy-with-food or temperature-with-drift task.
  - Sarosiek 2024, "Homeostasis as a foundation for adaptive and emotional artificial intelligence", Philosophical Problems in Science 77:41–54. A philosophy essay with no formal content and an unreliable reference list; not to be cited. Closer technical matches found by title, not read: arXiv 2507.04998 (linking homeostasis to RL) and arXiv 2512.22200 (EILS).
- Geist et al. 2022, "Concave utility RL: the mean-field game viewpoint", AAMAS, arXiv 2106.03787. CURL (max of a concave function of the occupancy) is a potential mean-field game with reward ∇F: concavity equals monotonicity, maximisers equal Nash equilibria, Hazan et al. is fictitious play is Frank–Wolfe, and online mirror descent (softmax of accumulated Q under the current population's reward) is faster. Naming: convex RL (Zahavy et al., min of a convex f) and CURL (max of a concave F) are the same class with F = −f, both convex programs; general-utility RL is the umbrella name (Zhang et al. 2020 coined it but assume F concave; non-concave F is treated in Zhang et al. 2021 and Barakat et al. 2023, entries below). Ours is the other sign: we minimise a concave H[dK], equivalently maximise the convex −H[dK], a non-convex program outside both names, so the game is anti-monotone and crowd-seeking (the Gibbs reward E_K ln q pays for outcomes the run already produces; MaxEnt's −ln ρ is crowd-averse). What transfers: the global optimum is a Nash, but not conversely, so MM fixed points are Nash equilibria and local optima; averaging steps are wrong for us, the full step is monotone; our tabular NPG actor is their OMD; exploitability φ(π) ≤ F* − F(μ_π) by convexity (our derivation), a cheap lower bound on suboptimality that is zero at every MM fixed point, so a diagnostic, not a certificate. Their stationary-occupancy footnote matches our average-reward occupancy. None of their guarantees transfer.
- Zhang, Koppel, Bedi, Szepesvári & Wang 2020, "Variational policy gradient method for RL with general utilities", NeurIPS, arXiv 2007.02151. Names the field but assumes F concave. Their chain rule (eq. 3.3), ∇_θ F(λ(θ)) = Σ_{s,a} ∂F/∂λ_sa ∇_θ λ_sa, is the policy gradient with pseudo-reward ∇F, which for us is the Gibbs reward E_K ln p. They estimate ∇F through the Fenchel dual as a saddle point, which needs concavity. For our convex F the variational form is v2's Gibbs identity −H = max_q E ln q, a joint maximisation over (π, q), not a saddle point, which is why MM is a joint ascent (our reading). Global optimality of stationary points via hidden convexity (the policy–occupancy bijection, their Theorem 4.2) needs concave F and does not hold for us.
- Zhang, Ni, Yu, Szepesvári & Wang 2021, TSIVR-PG, NeurIPS, arXiv 2102.08607; Barakat, Fatkhullin & He 2023, ICML, arXiv 2306.01854. Variance-reduced policy gradient for general utilities: Õ(ε^-3) samples to an ε-stationary policy for general, possibly non-concave, F, the guarantee that applies to us; Õ(ε^-2) to global optimality when F is concave. Both rest on a smoothness assumption (Barakat et al. Assumption 4.2, from Zhang et al. 2020 and 2021): ∇_λ F bounded in sup-norm and Lipschitz. Ours violates it where it matters: ∂F/∂d(s,a) = E_{K(·|s,a)} ln p(z) + 1 diverges when a pair emits outcomes the run's marginal barely produces, the concentrated regime the optimum sits in. Their rates need a floor on p, as Hazan et al. and Geist et al. smooth the entropy.
- Barakat, Chakraborty, Yu, Tokekar & Bedi 2025, NeurIPS, arXiv 2410.04108. Global optimality of policy gradient in general-utility RL via gradient domination, with function approximation of the occupancy by maximum likelihood. Concave utilities only; relevant to us for the function-approximation machinery, not the guarantees.
- Zahavy et al. 2021, "Reward is enough for convex MDPs". Occupancy polytopes for average and discounted cases, Fenchel-dual meta-algorithm. We borrow their definitions; our functional is concave, so their guarantees do not transfer. Added 23 September: our MM loop is their linearised-reward sequence (and the Frank–Wolfe sequence of Hazan et al.) with the sign of the objective flipped. Credit the loop to them; what is ours is monotone descent in the concave case, the collapse to gain-scaled SARSA on the reward channel, and the gain threshold.
- Hazan et al. 2019, provably efficient maximum-entropy exploration. Opposite sign of our objective, same occupancy view.

- Puterman 1994. Average-reward and discounted occupancy polytopes, policy improvement in the unichain case.
- Kakade 2001; Agarwal et al. 2021. Natural policy gradient for tabular softmax, the actor step we use.
- Wentworth 2021, "Fixing the good regulator theorem" (LessWrong / Alignment Forum). With imperfect information an optimal regulator is a function of the posterior over the system state; with information arriving before the task is known, the minimal memory *is* the posterior. Behavioural, not structural. We use it to justify the channel posterior in (2b) and the belief in (2c).

- Arumugam & Van Roy 2022, arXiv 2206.02072 and 2206.02025. Posterior sampling towards a value-equivalent learning target, not the true model. Closest prior art for "learning needs only the value-relevant posterior"; also a candidate refinement of (2b).
- Wang et al. 2022, Denoised MDP (arXiv 2206.15477); Liu et al. 2023, IFactor (arXiv 2306.06561). Split latents into controllable and reward-relevant factors. The reward-based version of Part 3's question.
- Huang et al. 2022, "Action-sufficient state representation learning", arXiv 2110.05721. Same name as the companion draft's notion, different and reward-based; the text must say which is meant.
- Ivashkov, Balestriero & Schölkopf 2026, "Sensorimotor world models", arXiv 2606.20104. Inverse dynamics as the only anti-collapse regulariser in a latent world model. The concrete action-sufficiency method Part 3 tests against.
- Biehl, Guckelsberger, Salge, Smith & Polani 2018, "Free energy, empowerment, and predictive information compared" (GSO'18); extended as "Expanding the active inference landscape", Frontiers in Neurorobotics 12:45, arXiv 1806.08083. One formalism for all three: an intrinsic motivation is a functional of the Bayesian complete posterior (predictive factor times posterior over environment and parameters) and an open-loop future action sequence, with argmax or softmax selection; a special case is Hutter's Bayesian universal RL agent. Expected free energy, intrinsic part (their eq. 80) = −H(S|E) + I(S:Θ), plus the extrinsic KL to a preference prior p_d (eq. 73); empowerment is Klyubin's capacity on the predictive law (eq. 107); predictive information is I(near future : far future) of the sensor stream (eq. 112). Against ours, with our Z for their S and c = −ln p_d: risk plus ambiguity is E[c] − H(Z) + H(Z|E) = E[c] − I(Z;E) (our derivation), while our tilted objective is E[c] + H(Z). Both charge the noise H(Z|E); EFE rewards the rest of H(Z), ours charges it. So EFE escapes the dark room through preferences and by seeking informative observations, ours only through a persistent disturbance. Its parameter information gain I(S:Θ) is the bonus of our dropped static J_exp; on the belief MDP we need no bonus. All three evaluate action sequences over a short horizon, not a policy's occupancy, so none has our concave-over-polytope structure. Their Bayesian empowerment sees the posterior only through the predictive law, the crux of our empowerment note. They also note Thompson sampling cannot serve a Θ-dependent motivation, as we found for J_exp. Pointer, not read: Little & Sommer 2013 compare predictive information with entropy minimisation (cited in their Sec. 7.3.4).
- Friston 2009, "The free-energy principle: a rough guide to the brain?", Trends in Cognitive Sciences 13:293–301 (cited by SMiRL). The principle's stated motivation is our objective: agents "minimize the entropy of their sensory states", and under ergodicity that entropy is the long-run time average of surprise −ln p(y|m); the "equilibrium density of an ensemble of agents" must have low entropy. Two refinements from our side: the time average of −ln p(y|m) under the agent's model m is a cross-entropy, equal to the entropy only when the model is the true long-run law, which is v2's Gibbs identity and the MM loop; and his "sensory states" are our outcome under the identity channel. Free energy then bounds surprise from above. The active-inference machinery that follows (expected free energy, below) does not minimise this entropy.
- Friston, Daunizeau & Kiebel 2009, "Reinforcement learning or active inference?", PLoS ONE 4:e6421 (cited by SMiRL). Mountain car solved by free-energy minimisation with no reward, value or utility: preferences are prior expectations. The claim we make on the reward channel, reached by putting the reward into priors instead of into a channel.
- Friston, Samothrakis & Montague 2012, "Active inference and agency: optimal control without cost functions", Biological Cybernetics 106:523–541 (cited by Biehl et al.). POMDP control as active inference, with costs absorbed into prior beliefs over transitions and terminal states. The control-as-inference reading of the same move.
- Friston, Rigoli, Ognibene, Mathys, FitzGerald & Pezzulo 2015, "Active inference and epistemic value", Cognitive Neuroscience 6:187–214 (cited by Biehl et al.). Defines the expected free energy of a policy and splits it into extrinsic value, expected utility under prior preferences, and epistemic value, information gain about hidden states. The source of the risk-plus-ambiguity term that rewards I(Z;E) in the Biehl et al. entry.
- Friston, FitzGerald, Rigoli, Schwartenbeck, O'Doherty & Pezzulo 2016, "Active inference and learning", Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews 68:862–879 (cited by SMiRL). Epistemic and pragmatic value tied to ambiguity and risk; habits emerge from sequential policy optimisation; the key distinction drawn is belief-free versus belief-based schemes, which is our belief-MDP point in their vocabulary.
- Friston, Lin, Frith, Pezzulo, Hobson & Ondobaka 2017, "Active inference, curiosity and insight", Neural Computation 29:2633–2683 (cited by Biehl et al.). Adds the parameter information gain ("novelty") to the expected free energy; learning structure from few observations through curious sampling. The bonus that our belief MDP replaces.
- Ueltzhöffer 2018, "Deep active inference", Biological Cybernetics 112:547–573; Tschantz, Baltieri, Seth & Buckley 2020, "Scaling active inference", IJCNN; Tschantz, Millidge, Seth & Buckley 2020, "Reinforcement learning through active inference", arXiv 2002.12636 (all cited by SMiRL). Deep and model-based implementations. Ueltzhöffer minimises a variational bound on average sensory surprise, "motivated by a homeostatic argument", the closest in spirit to ours; Tschantz et al. 2020b define the free energy of the expected future and show it bounds RL objectives, with results on sparse and no-reward control. The deep-RL baselines if Part 2 or 3 is compared with active inference. SMiRL's text also cites Annabi et al. 2020, which its reference list does not show; not traced.
